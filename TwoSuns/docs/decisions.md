# Two Suns decision log (ADRs)

Every durable design decision, newest last. [`spec.md`](spec.md) says what the product is; this file says why. Add an ADR at the end for any decision a future contributor would otherwise re-litigate. Mark old ADRs **Superseded** or **Amended**, never delete.

**Evidence levels used below.** *Owner choice*: the owner decided, nothing tests it. *Desk research*: the research report and notes, no code ran. *Simulator test*: a unit or screen-fit test that passed in the SDK 9.2.0 simulator (124 tests, 2026-09-27; the tests do not check pixels). Most of this has not run on a watch — ADR-005 and ADR-019 are the exceptions, both with real FR965 evidence cited in their own entries; treat every other ADR as simulator-only unless it says otherwise. Each ADR also says what would reverse it.

## ADR-022: Weather row in Pro

**Status: Proposed, BUILT (UNRELEASED). The owner overrode the weather non-goal in chat on 2026-10-03, chose the direction below and approved the mockup's look the same day (`docs/archive/weather-mockup.html`); built the same day, simulator-grade evidence only; nothing uploaded. The ADR stays Proposed with the rest of the Free + Pro pair (ADR-020).** Source: `../reports/Two Suns temperature research.md` (Rev 2, Rev 3) and `../research_notes/Two Suns temperature research/`.

**Decision.** Pro gets one new row, the weather row, between the time and the Body Battery band. Free is unchanged.
- **Now cell (the only coloured part).** A condition icon and **one number, the feels-like temperature** (`Weather.getCurrentConditions().feelsLikeTemperature`, SDK: "the wind chill or heat index", Celsius; converted for `System.getDeviceSettings().temperatureUnits`, shown as `15°`, no letter, no word). The actual temperature is not shown. Icon colour is fixed per icon type, never per value (studio rule: category-keyed colour is allowed, value-keyed colour is a verdict): sun and bolt `#FFFF55`, cloud and snow `#FFFFFF`, rain `#00AAFF`. **The number is one hue, `#FFFF55`, whatever the condition** (changed after the design review: a number that took the icon's hue made a storm number as cheerful as a clear one and a cloudy number white like the time). The lead icon is 1.4 times an ahead icon (43 px against 31 on 454, as in the approved mockup), except in the stacked next-day cell, where the weekday sits over an ahead-sized icon. Both hexes are 64-colour-safe; `#00AAFF` is 8.2 : 1 on black, computed.
- **Ahead cells (mono, `#AAAAAA`).** Up to three condition icons from `Weather.getHourlyForecast()` at even steps from now to sunset, the hour under each in the watch's 12 or 24 hour format. Icon and hour only: the hourly forecast has condition, temperature, precipitation chance, wind, UV and cloud cover but **no per-hour feels-like** (SDK `HourlyForecast`), so a temperature there would mix two quantities.
- **After sunset, and before sunrise:** the row shows the next daylight day from `Weather.getDailyForecast()`. **Only after sunset** it carries a chevron and the weekday; before sunrise it is today, which the date row already names, so the cell has no label. The label: a chevron and the weekday (from the date words the Pro date row already has; the chevron marks it as the next day, owner choice 2026-10-03, no new word to translate), condition icon, high and low, then up to three hourly icons only where the hourly list reaches that day. No night forecast.
- **Seven icons** (clear, partly cloudy, cloudy, rain, storm, snow, fog) drawn from primitives, as `DESIGN.md` requires (no bitmaps). The 54 `Weather.CONDITION_*` values of SDK 9.2 (all since API 3.2.0) are folded onto them in `TwoSunsWeatherKind`; windy and unknown, and any value newer than the list, have no icon: it is hidden, never guessed. The grey (mono) partly-cloudy icon draws its cloud over a black knockout so the sun and cloud stay apart.
- **Fit ladder (measured).** The row is the full two-line row (icon plus hour label) where the stack fits, otherwise a **compact one-line row** the height of the date row (icon, number, three ahead icons, no hour labels), otherwise gone. When the next-day row is wider than 75% of the chord, **the low goes first**, then ahead cells (`TwoSunsWeatherRow.lowKept`, `weatherNextDayDropsTheLowWhenTheRowIsWide`). Order of giving way, **as built**: the full row steps down to compact first, then the date drops, then the curve, then the row, then the sun line (ADR-016, rows follow font heights, amended). The first draft of this ADR put the date before compact; that would have dropped the date on the 218 px screen, which the owner's approved mockup keeps. Spare height from `twoSunsLayoutReport` (container run, 2026-10-03; row heights from the report, icon 16 px or larger, label as tall as the date row). The rows as built are 68 px (454), 54 (360), 35 (218, 240) and 19 for the compact row on 218; `weatherRowFormFollowsTheScreen` asserts compact with the date on 218 px and the full row with the date on 240 px and larger:

  | Screen | Span | Stack today | Spare | Two-line row + gap | Result |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 218 (`fr255s`) | 158 | 123 | 35 | 38 (16 + 19 + 3) | **compact** (22) |
  | 240 (`fenix7s`) | 172 | 128 | 44 | 38 (35 + 3), as built | two-line |

  Not measured: 466 and 448 by 486 screens. `everyStateFitsThisDisplay` (every state, now with four weather states) passes on the devices listed under Evidence.
- **Empty and stale.** A read that fails or comes back empty keeps the last good data (`TwoSunsWeatherSource`), so a flaky read does not make the row, and with it the whole stack, jump; the age rules below still drop what has aged out. A remaining jump (the time moves 37 px on 454 when the row appears or goes) happens when the setting is turned off, when the data has aged out (3 hours) or never came, and after sunset when Garmin has no entry for tomorrow. No forecast, or forecast entries older than the current hour: those cells are not drawn. `observationTime` older than 3 hours (proposal): the now cell is not drawn. Nothing at all: the row is absent and the face re-stacks to today's layout. Never an invented or greyed value. If `feelsLikeTemperature` is null but `temperature` is not, the proposal is to show `temperature` (owner may prefer hiding it).
- **Not drawn always-on.** The AMOLED always-on frame stays time, Body Battery value and sun sentence.
- **Setting.** One list setting `Weather` (Off, On), key appended, default On in Pro only. A shipped key never changes (house rule).
- **Code.** `Weather` stays un-imported and is named in full inside `(:pro)` functions; a `(:free)` twin returns null. No new permission (compiler: `Weather` needs none; Pro already calls `Toybox.Weather.getCurrentConditions()` in `TwoSunsSources`). Readings are gathered in `TwoSunsSources`/`TwoSunsReadings`, drawn from `TwoSunsState` (house rules).
- **Accent roster.** The planned accent ids 8 (yellow `#FFFF55`) and 6 (cyan) must not be admitted while the weather hues are `#FFFF55` and `#00AAFF`; a test `noAccentIsAWeatherHue` goes with the build. The default sky accent `#55AAFF` is one channel step from rain blue `#00AAFF`, and autumn `#FFAA00` from the sun yellow. With the number now one yellow hue, rain blue only appears on 2 px drop strokes inside a white cloud, so the confusion risk is small; it stays a **look gate for a real screen** (an accent test that rejects one-step neighbours would fail the shipped default, so none was added).

**Design review (watch-design-reviewer, fresh context, 2026-10-03; read from code and layout numbers, no pixels).** Applied: lead icon 1.4 times an ahead icon (the build had drawn them equal); 15 more conditions mapped; mono partly-cloudy knockout and a lower bolt; thinner chevron; sun rays computed in one multiply; larger flakes; compact ahead icons the row height (19, not 15); the weather row re-measured after the curve re-plan; the number one hue; the chevron only after sunset; the low dropped first on a wide row; the last good data held. **Not applied, owner's call:** reserving the row's height so the time never moves; dropping the date row on the 454 px screen whenever the weather row is full (the stack is 318 of 326 px). **Cannot be judged without a screen:** stroke rendering at 16 to 31 px, whether yellow pulls the eye from the white time, glare on MIP, the real inputs.
**Why.** The owner wants current conditions and the day's weather at a glance, daytime only. The hourly forecast reaches about 12 hours (forum, not in the SDK docs, unverified on a watch), so it covers most of the day from mid-morning; early morning gets a shorter strip, and the daily forecast covers the next day. A coloured now cell with a mono strip keeps one focal weather read and leaves the time as the hero.
**Alternatives.** Thermal ticks on the ring (temperature trend, no conditions): demoted to an optional later add-on. A hairline trace: rejected, it wandered into the ring in the mockup. Numbers only in the date row: kept as the fallback shape if the owner drops the row. Actual plus feels-like temperature: the owner chose feels-like only. Per-hour computed feels-like (wind chill or heat index from temperature, wind, humidity): not built, a modelled value that could disagree with Garmin's own number.
**Built.** `TwoSunsWeather` (the row's words, shared so `TwoSunsState` compiles in both tiers), `TwoSunsWeatherData`, `TwoSunsWeatherKind`, `TwoSunsWeatherPlan`, `TwoSunsWeatherIcons` and `TwoSunsWeatherRow` (all `(:pro)`), `TwoSunsFrame` (weather mode, the step-down), `TwoSunsLayout.rows` and `stackHeight` (a weather row argument), `TwoSunsSources.readWeather` (cached 5 minutes), setting `Weather` (`TwoSunsConfig.KEY_WEATHER`, Pro list setting, default On). 19 new Pro-only tests (Pro 154, Free 67 unchanged). The `setting_weather` title is in every language folder as a **machine draft, unreviewed** (the owner's call, publish-checklist F11).
**Evidence.** SDK 9.2.0 docs (`Weather`, `CurrentConditions`, `HourlyForecast`, `DailyForecast`, `System.DeviceSettings`); the layout table above (simulator); `docs/archive/weather-mockup.html` and its browser screenshot (a drawing with sample data, not a wrist); the built row's boxes in `twoSunsLayoutReport` (live state from the simulator's canned weather). **Simulator only: canned weather. No screenshot of the built row exists** (the environment cannot capture the simulator). **Seen on the owner's FR965 wear check (2026-10-03 evening, reports): the weather row shows and works (`> Sun 17 10` after sunset), and the Weather toggle works in Customize (B8, after the Customize fix in ADR-023); B1 to B7 and B9 to B11 are not yet reported.** **Seen on the FR965 (probe photos, 2026-10-03 21:07 to 21:10; the round screen cuts the left and right of the lines):** the hourly list had 12 entries, 60 minutes apart, the first at the **current hour** (21:00 at 21:07) and the last at 08:00 the next day; the daily list had 5 entries stamped at **local midnight** (`2T21:00Z`, `3T21:00Z`, `4T21:00Z`, `5T21:00Z` in UTC+3, today first), so tomorrow is the second entry and the next-day lookup finds it by local day; the observation was 18 to 20 minutes old; conditions were plain codes (1 partly cloudy, 2 mostly cloudy, 20 cloudy); temperatures arrive as Celsius floats converted from whole Fahrenheit (14.444445 is 58 F), so whole-degree rounding is right. At 21:00 the hourly list reaches only 08:00 tomorrow, so after sunset the next-day row has its daily cell and, with the 10, 13 and 16 o'clock targets 90 minutes or more away, no ahead cells (as designed). A later photo set (about 22:22): the observation had moved from 20:50 to 21:50 and the hourly list from a 21:00 start to a 22:00 start in one step (n still 12), the daily conditions had changed with it: the weather is re-issued about hourly, the list's first entry being the full hour after the observation; the plan already drops an entry whose hour has ended. Not seen: the `k` values (cut by the screen edge, inferred from the stamps), `f` against Garmin's Weather widget on a day when they differ (they were both 15 at 21:10: the widget said "Feels Like 15°"), whether the list moves at the clock hour between syncs, Bluetooth off. Not verified anywhere else: the number of hourly entries on other watches, how often the data refreshes, the daily `k` values on a watch (the next-day row accepts a stamp at exactly UTC midnight, the simulator's form, as its UTC date and any other stamp as its local day; `weatherUtcMidnightStampsMatchTheirOwnDate`), behaviour when the phone is away or the Garmin weather widget is missing, how the icons look on a wrist or in sun on MIP.
**Amended 2026-10-04 (owner, after reading `11 3 6` on the wrist):** on a 12 hour clock the ahead hours carry `a` or `p` (`11a`, `3p`, `6p`, as Garmin's own hourly view writes them), inline, so the row's height is unchanged; a 24 hour clock stays bare (`11`, `15`, `18`). The labels grow from 34 to up to 51 px at 454 px, so the widest next-day row (three-digit Fahrenheit, `-40°` low, `12p` hours) now keeps two ahead cells, not three; the compact row has no labels and is unchanged. Simulator-only; the look is not yet seen on the wrist.

**Seen on the owner's FR965 (wear check, photos 2026-10-03 23:18 and 2026-10-04 08:50; `device-test/TwoSuns-weather-RESULTS.md`):** the row works in the morning (icon, number, three ahead cells with 12 h labels clear of the icons, time still the largest read, partly-cloudy shows sun and cloud) and after sunset (arrow, weekday matching the date, tomorrow's icon, yellow high, grey low kept on 454 px, one hourly cell); Weather Off and On work in Customize (B8). Probe on the same watch: hourly list 12 entries 60 min apart from the current hour, daily list 5 local-midnight stamps. Not yet seen: tonight's Monday cell (16/12 expected), the glance test in sun (B4), rain blue (B5), before-sunrise row (B7), Fahrenheit (B9), Bluetooth off (B10), always-on (B11), the owner's look verdicts (Q5 to Q8).

## ADR-023: Watch battery row, Body Battery bolt gauge, next-day arrow

**Status: Proposed, BUILT (UNRELEASED). Owner, 2026-10-03, after wearing the weather row: the `>` before the next-day weekday looks poor and should be a vector icon; the watch battery should show at the top, above the date, with an option to hide it; the Body Battery glyph should be easier to tell from a watch battery. The owner chose the bolt gauge from the mockup (`docs/archive/battery-mockup.html`, rev 5) and took the recommended options for the rest. Simulator evidence only; no screenshot of the build exists.**

**Decision.**
- **Watch battery row (Pro only, setting `Battery`, On by default).** One muted row, `#AAAAAA`, centred above the first row of the stack: a classic battery (outline, nub, fill to the charge) and the whole percent (`System.getSystemStats().battery`, rounded, clamped 0 to 100). It sits one gap above the stack's first row, in the strip between the stack and the ring, so it **never moves another row**. It is drawn only where its ink (65% of the label font's height) fits the round chord; elsewhere it is not drawn (no drop-order change). Measured in the container (`twoSunsLayoutReport`, live state): drawn on 280, 390, 416, 454 and 466 px screens; not drawn on 360 px (`fr265s`, `venusq2`). The widest test states carry "100%"; `everyStateFitsThisDisplay` checks its box against the content circle and every other box. Not in the always-on frame. No charging mark, no low-battery colour (no verdicts; same rule as ADR-008 for Body Battery).
- **Body Battery glyph: a bolt gauge (both tiers).** A dim bolt (`#555555`) filled from the bottom to the level in the accent (`dim(accent)` below 30, ADR-008 amendment), the fill clipped to the bolt's own edges; hollow (a muted outline, no fill) when there is no number or it is stale (the shape carries the state as well as the colour, ADR-017). Height of the value font, 0.6 as wide. It replaces the level pill of ADR-017 (which beside a battery row read as a second battery). Free draws the same bolt: the glyph is shared code, so the Free look changes too.
- **Next-day marker: an arrow, not a character.** A shaft and a filled head, muted, drawn from primitives like the weather icons (a bitmap or SVG resource would be fixed-size per product, has the FR165 tint bug and breaks DESIGN.md's "primitives only"). Replaces the chevron of ADR-022.
- **On-watch Customize carries Weather and Battery** (found missing on the owner's wrist, 2026-10-03, checklist B14: ADR-022's Weather and this ADR's Battery had been added to the phone settings page and the properties but not to the hand-built Customize menu of ADR-019, and a sideloaded build has no phone page). Both are toggles now; `customizeMenuHasEveryProSetting` and a new `gen_settings.py --check` rule (every setting needs an `ITEM_` in `TwoSunsSettingsMenu`) stop the same omission.
- **New setting `Battery`** (Pro list setting, key appended, never changes once shipped); its title `setting_battery` ("Watch battery") is in every language folder as an **unreviewed machine draft** (publish-checklist F11).

**Why.** On the wrist the chevron read as a typed character beside vector icons; the owner wants the watch's charge at a glance without adding a row to the stack; and a Body Battery pill beside a battery glyph is two batteries. A bolt means "energy" and is nothing like a rectangle with a nub. **Cost, said plainly:** a bolt also means "charging" on many watches, and Garmin's own Body Battery icon is bolt-like; nothing here uses the words "Body Battery" (release-contract), but the look gate is whether the owner reads the bolt as energy on the wrist.
**Alternatives.** A ring gauge for Body Battery (the recommended option in the mockup; the owner chose the bolt); keeping the pill and giving the watch battery another form; a triangle or the chevron for the next-day marker; putting the watch battery in the date row; reserving a row for it (it would push the stack past the span on 454 px: 318 of 326 px with the weather row).
**Seen on the owner's FR965 (wear check, 2026-10-03 evening, photos and reports; `device-test/TwoSuns-weather-RESULTS.md`):** the battery row shows (B13), the Body Battery bolt reads as a bolt (B15), the next-day arrow is clear (B16), the Battery toggle works in Customize (B14, after the fix above), no crash so far (B12). Not yet seen: the row against the ring in sun, the bolt on small screens.
**Evidence.** `twoSunsLayoutReport` boxes and 154 Pro / 67 Free tests (four new, `TwoSunsBatteryRowTest`) in the simulator; the mockup screenshots. Not seen: any pixel of the build, the bolt at 11 px wide on the 218 px screen, the battery row against the ring on a wrist.
**Reversed by.** The owner not reading the bolt as energy on the wrist (back to the pill or the ring); the battery row crowding the ring on a real screen; Garmin giving a face a way to draw its own icons.

**Amendment 2026-10-05 (owner, design critique, ROADMAP 13.13 and 13.16; simulator only).** The bolt is solid in the current battery colour, no longer a gauge: half grey, half colour read as "broken", and the number beside it already gives the level. Hollow when stale or empty, unchanged. The watch battery row (and the weather row, ADR-022) are Off by default for new installs, so the first face matches the listing; wearers who switched them on keep their setting.

**Amended 2026-10-05 (ADR-028, the rectangle track).** On a rectangle the watch battery row is drawn in its own strip at the top of the box (on both the Venu Sq 2 and the Venu X1), and switching it on can step the time down a size: an exception to "never moves another row", which round screens keep. The "not drawn on 360 px" line now means the round 360 px screens only. Screenshots of the built row exist since 2026-10-04 (simulator).

## ADR-001: Concept

**Decision.** One face answers one question: how much light, and how much energy, do I have left today? A 24-hour ring around the bezel is the sky's sun, a 24-hour curve under the time is the watch's Body Battery, and one sentence at the bottom says how much daylight is left or when the sun returns. The time is the hero.
**Why.** No rival relates the two: 282 of 1,378 surveyed faces mention both, almost all as 14-to-40-field dashboards; none is built around the pair.
**Evidence.** Desk research: `reports/Body Battery and sun face research.md` §1 and §2. The build's look is the spec's recommended direction and the owner has **not** approved it ([`../DESIGN.md`](../DESIGN.md)).
**Reversed by.** The owner replacing the look with a mock-up (then `spec.md` and `DESIGN.md` change, the concept stands), or a decision that a face should not carry Body Battery at all.

## ADR-002: Price

**Status: Superseded 2026-10-04 by ADR-020 (the Free + Pro ladder).** The owner approved the ladder on 2026-10-04 and retired the day-45 review below: the paid app is never flipped to free, the Free twin replaces that idea. The price (USD 1.99 documented, $2.25 shown in the store) is **Superseded by [ADR-026](#adr-026-price-the-250-tier-for-every-paid-app) (price: the $2.50 tier for every paid app)**. The text below is the history.

**Decision.** Paid, USD 1.99 at first submission (owner, confirmed 2026-09-27) — Garmin's first paid price step; a lower figure the owner initially named (USD 1.90) turned out not to be an offered price, since Garmin does not allow custom prices, only fixed steps. This lands on the same tier as Days To Go, which the owner had originally wanted to avoid, but is the nearest real option. The review cadence follows Days To Go's rules ([`../../DaysToGo/docs/decisions.md`](../../DaysToGo/docs/decisions.md) ADR-002): one review 45 days after store approval, and the success test at day 60 ([`spec.md`](spec.md) "Success and stop test"). Days To Go's proposed flip rule (fewer than 5 sales in 45 days and a download bucket of 10 or lower) is the working proposal here too; the owner has not confirmed it for this app.
**Why.** Owner choice, 2026-09-26 ("Paid like Days To Go"). Risk on record: about 75 paid Body Battery or sun faces, 2 of them at 1,000+ downloads. **Night & Day** (50,000 downloads) lists free and unlocks on the developer's own site; its 1-star reviews are about that. Do not copy it. Garmin's app trials do not work for watch faces, so there is no native trial.
**Evidence.** Owner choice; desk research (`market_and_pricing.md`).
**Reversed by.** The day-45 review. Details and the "email developer support first, never cancel the merchant account" rule: Days To Go ADR-002. Price review due: not set until approval.

## ADR-003: Sunrise and sunset come from Complications

**Decision.** Today's sunrise and sunset are `Complications` SUNRISE and SUNSET (seconds since local midnight, 0 to 86399, truncated to minutes): Garmin's own numbers, the ones the watch's Sunrise/Sunset glance shows, in the watch's local time, with no location. `Weather.getSunrise` and `getSunset` are **not** the primary source; they are allowed only as a cross-check and as the tier B fallback (1.1), and never without a location. The code today does not call them at all.
**Why.** Reviewers compare a face with Garmin's glance, and Complications need no location. The rivals' "it shows UTC" bugs are not explained by the Weather API (below), so there was no API defect to design around.
**Evidence.** Desk research plus simulator probes, `research_notes/Body Battery and sun face research/platform.md` §3 and `probe/sim-sun-date-sweep-hourly.log`. An hourly sweep (the simulator's clock was UTC+3) asked `Weather.getSunrise/getSunset` for every hour from 26 September 18:00Z to 27 September 02:00Z for Hawaii, Sydney and London. The returned pair flipped at 26 September 21:00Z, which is 00:00 on the watch's clock, not at 00:00Z: the API follows the watch's **local** calendar day. **An earlier reading was wrong and is retracted:** a first sweep in 6-hour steps spanned both the local and the UTC midnight and was read as "UTC date". Simulator only; whether real firmware behaves the same is what the on-watch probe log (an `A` line across local midnight) records. The Complication values in the simulator are canned (sunrise 54535, sunset 11599, a sunset earlier than the sunrise), so they prove parsing and the after-midnight rule, not Garmin's numbers.
**Reversed by.** The device compare: if the Complication values differ from the native glance by more than 1 minute, or are null on the owner's watch, the calculation becomes primary and this ADR is superseded. If there is then no location, tier A has no sun data and the owner decides ([`plan.md`](archive/plan.md) "Stop and ask").

## ADR-004: Own NOAA calculation, and tomorrow's sunrise

**Decision.** Everything the sky needs that Garmin does not give is our own calculation (`TwoSunsSun`, pure `Float` functions): sunrise, sunset, solar noon, civil twilight (zenith 96 degrees), golden hour (84 degrees), evaluated at the local noon of the local date, zenith 90.833 degrees for sunrise and sunset. After sunset the face announces tomorrow's sunrise as calculated tomorrow plus (Garmin's today minus calculated today), so it keeps Garmin's offset. No copy of any library: the only public Monkey C one is LGPL.
**Why.** Garmin gives no tomorrow, dawn, dusk or golden hour. The correction keeps the two sources consistent to the minute.
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `TwoSunsSunReferenceTest` (generated by `tools/gen_sun_tests.py` from `reference_sun_times.tsv`): 27 place-and-date reference tests against the US Naval Observatory, tolerance 2 minutes where the Observatory lists an event (rise, set, civil twilight; solar noon compared around the clock). Polar rows assert "no event" and the sun's kind; the two polar-circle transition days (Tromso 18 May and the day the midnight sun starts) check solar noon only, so a difference of one day at the transitions is accepted, as the spec says. The maths runs on days since J2000 in 32-bit floats because a Julian date does not fit a `Float`. Not verified: that a watch's float behaviour equals the simulator's; the calculation's agreement with Garmin's own numbers (the simulator's `Weather` day was 7 minutes longer than the Observatory's for London on 27 September, and which zenith Garmin uses is undocumented).
**Reversed by.** The device compare showing our calculation and Garmin's differing by more than the tolerance in a way the tomorrow correction does not absorb.

## ADR-005: Location order and the Positioning permission

**Decision.** The location sources are read in this order: `Activity.getActivityInfo().currentLocation`, then the Weather observation location, then `Position.getInfo()`, then the saved place (ADR-006). The manifest declares `Positioning`, **now confirmed, not provisional.** `Position.getInfo()` is isolated in one function, `TwoSunsSources.positionLocation`, and its one entry in `updatePlace()`, kept that way even though the permission is confirmed, so a future reversal stays a two-deletion change. Every location source is guarded; a watch without one just gives null and the face says "No place yet".
**Why.** On the owner's FR965 (2026-09-27, real watch, not the simulator): `Activity.currentLocation` stayed null even right after a GPS run was started and discarded (Q1 = no); the Weather observation location stayed null throughout, with or without a GPS activity (Q2 = no); `Position.getInfo()` returned a location immediately at first look, with no GPS activity run first — a cached fix (Q3 = yes). Per the decision table (`plan.md` phase 1): Q1 and Q2 no, Q3 yes → keep Positioning. Simulator behaviour for Q3 was the opposite (always null without an activity); the real watch differs.
**Evidence.** Simulator probes (`platform.md` §1 and §2) plus the on-watch probes, run 2026-09-27 (`../../device-test/LocationProbe-P.prg` with the permission, `LocationProbe-N.prg` without; results in `../../device-test/LocationProbe-RESULTS.md`). M1 (first look) and M2 (after a GPS activity, discarded) were run on N; M3 (outdoors, over time) and M4 (overnight log) were not, and neither was the native-glance comparison (Q4) — none of them can change this call (Q3 already answers it), so they are left for later, before store submission.
**Reversed by.** Nothing found so far reverses it. If a later run of M3 or M4 ever shows Activity or Weather giving a location without Positioning, drop the permission, delete `positionLocation()` and its entry in `updatePlace()`, and say "no location permission" on the listing.
**Amended 2026-10-01** (ADR-020, the Free + Pro ladder, accepted 2026-10-04): the Positioning permission, `positionLocation` and the whole location order are Pro only; the Free manifest has `ComplicationSubscriber` alone and Free reads no location. Pro is unchanged.


## ADR-006: The remembered place

**Decision.** A place is `[latitude, longitude]` rounded to 0.1 degree (about 11 km), kept in `Application.Storage` under the key `place`, the only thing the app stores. A fresher source replaces it only when it is more than 0.1 degree away (plus a 0.001 epsilon) in latitude or longitude; since both values are already rounded, that is in practice a step of 0.2 degree, and jitter causes no writes. A fix of exactly (0, 0) is rejected as a no-fix placeholder, and so is anything outside +-90 or +-180. Values read back from Storage are accepted as any numeric type (Float, Double or Number) and re-validated. A failing store only loses the memory. It is never sent anywhere.
**Why.** Sun times need minutes per quarter degree, so 0.1 degree is enough and is not a precise location. Storing little, writing rarely and validating on read protects the face from a bad or foreign value.
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `TwoSunsPlaceTest` (rounding, source order, replace only when moved, empty, (0, 0), storage types). Nothing about a real fix.
**Reversed by.** ADR-005 removing every location source that could fill it (then the key is unused), or a listing/privacy decision that the face must not store a place.
**Amended 2026-10-01** (ADR-020, the Free + Pro ladder, accepted 2026-10-04): Pro only. The Free build has no remembered place and uses no `Application.Storage` at all.


## ADR-007: Always-on

**Decision.** On AMOLED watches that require burn-in protection the sleep frame is the time, the Body Battery value and the sun sentence, in a dim grey, `#5C5C5C` (amended 2026-09-27 and 2026-10-04, below; ADR-027, always-on text is a dim grey), the block stepping across a 3 by 3 grid every minute; no ring, no curve, no glyph, no date. The time is always two font sizes below whatever awake just picked, derived at draw time from awake's own choice (amended 2026-09-27, below) — not a fixed size. MIP watches show the full face at all times. The step is 35 permille of the shorter screen side (`BURN_IN_STEP_PERMILLE`), 15 px on 454 by integer arithmetic.
**Why.** A commonly-cited version of Garmin's limit is at most 10% of pixels lit and none continuously for more than a few minutes; copied from Days To Go without a primary citation in either app (see `knowledge/platform-facts.md` in `watch-design-kit`, tagged unverified). Moving the block every minute keeps any one pixel in the lit stroke for at most a minute either way.
**Evidence.** The underlying "10%" figure is uncited, not Garmin-confirmed. Simulator test: the always-on frame fits at every one of the nine drift positions (`alwaysOnFrameFitsAtEveryDrift`). **Not measured:** lit-pixel share, ghosting, a night on a real AMOLED.
**Reversed by.** The always-on night check ([`publish-checklist.md`](status.md) gate 5): if the screen blanks or ghosts, raise `BURN_IN_STEP_PERMILLE` or shrink the time, then repeat.
**Amended 2026-09-27** (`watch-design-reviewer`, `watch-design-kit`), two fixes:
1. The always-on text was `#555555`, 2.8:1 against true black — under this project's own ≥3:1 bar for a persistent/always-on colour. Changed to `#5555AA` (3.3:1, same value as the night track; safe to share because the ring never draws during sleep). `TwoSunsPalette.SLEEP_TEXT`.
2. The sleep time font was a fixed independent list (`SLEEP_TIME_FONTS`, starting at `FONT_NUMBER_MEDIUM`) that matched awake's font exactly whenever awake had already fallen back to `MEDIUM` — contradicting "two sizes smaller" on any screen where that happened. Now derived from awake's actual choice at draw time (`TwoSunsDraw.fontsBelow`), so it is genuinely two steps below awake on every screen, floored at the smallest available font.
Also corrected the code comments' claim that a pixel stays lit for "3 minutes"/"three updates": re-deriving the actual drift (`minute % BURN_IN_GRID`, `minute / BURN_IN_GRID % BURN_IN_GRID`) shows the block's x-offset changes every single minute, so the true dwell is one minute, not three — the code was already safer than documented, just documented wrong.

**Amended 2026-10-04 (ROADMAP 10.17, Garmin policies research; the text above is kept as written on 2026-09-26/27).** The "10%" figure is **not uncited any more: Garmin publishes it**, and the "Evidence" line above is superseded on that point. Garmin's AMOLED FAQ ("How do I Make a Watch Face for AMOLED Products?", also in the SDK docs, read 2026-10-04): burn-in protection runs when the watch face is in the foreground and the system is asleep, and "if more than 10% of the screen pixels are on or any pixel is on for longer than 3 minutes, the system will shut off the screen" (a pixel is on in any colour but black). Caveats: (1) **Garmin's pages disagree on the time**: that FAQ says 3 minutes, the Entry Points page says "AMOLED always active (version 1)" stops "any pixel from being enabled for more than four minutes", or more than 10% of the pixels; we design to the shorter figure. (2) **The pixel rule is the original Venu's.** The same FAQ: "Since the Venu 2, the rule for always-on is to use less than 10% of the screen's luminance", and Entry Points' "version 2" has only a 10% pixel limit with no dwell time; which product uses which rule is read at run time (`requiresBurnInProtection`, `System.getDisplayMode`), not from a list we hold. So "lit-pixel share" is the right measure only on the original Venu; on Venu 2 and later it is luminance, where a dim `#5555AA` counts for far less than white. (3) What remains unmeasured is this frame's own share: no heat-map run and no always-on night on a wrist (ROADMAP 3.1); the simulator's File > View Screen Heat Map is Garmin's tool. The dwell finding above (one minute) holds against both the 3 and the 4 minute figure. Garmin's watch-face page also says to avoid much white or blue: see the finding in `DESIGN.md` under "Always-on (AMOLED)" (the text colour, `#5555AA`).

**Amended 2026-10-05 (ADR-028, the rectangle track).** On a rectangle the always-on time is two steps below the grown awake time in a list that continues past `FONT_NUMBER_MILD` to `FONT_LARGE` and `FONT_MEDIUM`, so it is always smaller than awake. Round screens keep this ADR's rule.

## ADR-008: No verdicts on Body Battery

**Decision.** Body Battery is shown as a number and a neutral picture: never a mood, an emoji, advice, a threshold word or a good/bad colour scheme (red/amber/green). The face never shows the words good, low, rest or tired. The phone setting that hides the curve is labelled "Energy curve", not "Body Battery". "Body Battery" is Garmin's trademark: never in the store name, the icon or the brand, used only descriptively in the listing.
**Amended by ADR-023 (2026-10-03): the pill is now a bolt gauge** (same colours and the same hollow-when-stale rule).
**Amended 2026-09-27 (owner): the value, pill and current-point dot dim below 30** (`TwoSunsConfig.BATTERY_LOW_THRESHOLD`, `TwoSunsReadings.batteryAccentFor`) — the chosen accent turns to `TwoSunsPalette.dim(accent)`, the exact colour the sky ring already uses for daylight already gone, so "dim" means the same thing everywhere on the face. This is a real exception to "no colour by level," narrowly scoped: one brightness step, the same hue throughout (never a hue change, never red), no word, no icon, no threshold named anywhere in the UI. It exists because a flat accent colour created its own problem (a normal value read as "looks low" purely from the colour's own connotation, amber especially); two brightness states, keyed to the chosen hue, read as "less charge" without reading as "bad." The pill's fill length and the curve's height already carry the continuous level signal; this only adds a glance-level cue, and stops at two states because a third palette-safe step collapses most accents into the same grey already used for "stale," which would make "very low" and "no data" indistinguishable — rejected for that reason ([`DESIGN.md`](../DESIGN.md) "Energy curve, glyph and value").
**Why.** Reviewers of the Pokemon Sleep faces asked for background or colour choice (59 of 505) and to pick or turn off the mood (9), and one found the sad pose upsetting; Garmin's own Body Battery page says "the occasional low-energy day is no cause for alarm". The store's review guidelines forbid medical claims. The 2026-09-27 amendment: a real-device photo showed the flat amber accent misreading a normal value as low; brightness-of-one-hue was picked over a red/green scale specifically to avoid reintroducing that verdict risk.
**Evidence.** Desk research (`rival_reviews.md`, `garmin_sources.md`, `naming.md`). No test enforces the absence of a verdict WORD; `strings.xml` has no such words (checked by reading, and by `tools/check_strings.py` only for length). The two-tier dim IS tested: `TwoSunsReadingsTest.batteryAccentForDimsOnlyBelowTheThreshold` and `batteryAccentFollowsTheLevel`.
**Reversed by.** The owner. A three-tier or hue-based scheme needs a new palette design (the current one cannot go past two brightness states without colour collapse, see above).

## ADR-011: The 24-hour ring is wall-clock

**Decision.** The ring shows local wall-clock minutes 0 to 1440, noon at the top by default (a setting puts midnight at the top), clockwise. One degree is four minutes (marker and tick precision is about +-1 degree). On a daylight-saving change day the ring has a one-hour jump; documented, not fixed.
**Why.** It reads like a clock and matches the numbers on the face. The alternative (elapsed time) needs a zone database the watch face does not have.
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `TwoSunsRingPlanTest` (angles always in range, arcs, ticks, both orientations). The sun tests cover the London DST change days (29 March and 25 October) and New York 8 March, but that tests the sun times, not the drawn ring across the change.
**Reversed by.** A device wear day across a DST change showing a confusing ring.

## ADR-012: The local UTC offset is derived exactly

**Decision.** The offset is `(local day number - UTC day number) * 1440 + local minute - UTC minute` (`TwoSunsLocalTime.offsetBetween`), from one instant read twice with `Gregorian.info` and `Gregorian.utcInfo`. It is not wrapped to +-12 hours, so +14:00 works, and `System.getClockTime().timeZoneOffset` and `.dst` are not used.
**Why.** The Garmin docs say only "time offset from UTC in seconds" and "daylight savings time offset"; comparing two readings of the same instant needs no assumption. Comparing day numbers as well as clock times keeps +14:00 and -10:00 (the same clock time, 24 hours apart) from being mixed up. This replaces the spec's original "wrapped to +-12 h" rule.
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `TwoSunsLocalTimeTest`. Probe Q8 on a watch (does `timeZoneOffset` plus `dst` equal the derived difference?) has not been run and is not needed for the face.
**Reversed by.** Nothing planned; a watch where the two readings of one instant disagree in a way the arithmetic cannot absorb.

## ADR-013: Sky states and the calculation fallback

**Decision.** `TwoSunsSky.resolve` takes Garmin's sunrise and sunset first. If both are null but a place is known, the calculation fills in; a polar day or night comes from the calculation only. If a place is unknown, the sentence is "No place yet" when Complications exist and "No sun data" when they do not. If only one is null (a transition day) the missing time is taken from the calculation when there is one. A sunset earlier than the sunrise means the sunset is after local midnight (add 1440). After sunset the sentence announces tomorrow's sunrise per ADR-004; with no place it shows today's Garmin sunrise flagged as an estimate ("Sunrise ~06:41"); if the calculation says the sun does not rise tomorrow it says "No sunrise tomorrow"; if nothing is known it says "Sunset HH:MM" or "No sun data". **Known edge:** on a day whose sunset is after local midnight (Reykjavik in June), the minutes from 00:00 to that sunset read "before sunrise", because Garmin's pair describes the date that is starting, not the sun still up from yesterday.
**Why.** Every failure has a sentence, never a blank (the category's signature complaint is blank sun times). The edge is accepted because fixing it needs yesterday's pair.
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `TwoSunsSkyTest` (13 tests: boundaries, tomorrow's offset, estimate, after-midnight sunset, polar states, transition day, no place and no data, fallback to the calculation), `TwoSunsReadingsTest`. The polar and transition rows use the simulator's canned Complication values and the calculation only.
**Reversed by.** The device compare; a real Reykjavik-like day showing the edge is worse than expected.
**Amended 2026-10-01** (ADR-020, the Free + Pro ladder, accepted 2026-10-04): the Free build never has a place, so it never says "No place yet": Garmin's null pair is "No sun data" (`TwoSunsSky.noCalculationState`, `(:free)` twin). Polar day and night, twilight and tomorrow's own sunrise need the calculation and so are Pro only; Free after sunset shows today's Garmin sunrise flagged as an estimate ("Sunrise ~06:41"), the no-place wording of this ADR.


## ADR-014: Three wordings for the sun sentence

**Decision.** Each sun sentence has up to three wordings: full, shorter, shortest (`TwoSunsConfig.SKY_WORDING_LEVELS` = 2 levels below the full one). The layout takes the longest that fits the round chord at the row, stepping down in font as well. Sentences with no shorter form ("No place yet", "No sun data", "Sun is up") are kept to 14 characters or fewer in every language (`tools/check_strings.py`), and each last-resort wording with a time in it to 16 characters.
**Why.** The sun line sits low on the round screen where the chord is narrow. The strengthened screen-fit test failed on `fr265s` (360 px) and `venusq2` (320 by 360) with a single wording, before this fix.
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `everyStateFitsThisDisplay` on ten sizes, `skyLineShortWordings`, `stateListsSentencesLongestFirst`; `check_strings.py` passes on the current strings. The character limits are a proxy; `tools/fit_languages.sh` measures pixels and has **not been run**, so no translation has been fit-tested.
**Reversed by.** A translation that does not fit in `fit_languages.sh`, or a native reader rewriting the sentences.

## ADR-015: Body Battery data rules

**Decision.** The last 24 hours of `SensorHistory` Body Battery go into 96 buckets of 15 minutes by each sample's own `when` (never `getOldestSampleTime` or `getNewestSampleTime`); the newest sample in a bucket wins; only 0 to 100 is valid (127 means not worn and is dropped, so are null and negative values). A sample stamped up to 5 minutes ahead is clock skew and is kept in the last bucket; later is dropped. The value is **stale** when the newest sample is older than 60 minutes: the curve, the value and the glyph are muted, the dot and the glyph become outlines. The history is re-read at most every 300 seconds. Gaps stay gaps: no interpolation. If the watch has no history API (or reading it throws), the Complication BODY_BATTERY number is used when it is valid, with no curve. **If history exists but has no valid sample, the value is "--" and Garmin's number is not substituted.**
**Why.** Forum reports say the oldest and newest sample times do not match the samples. A wrong number is worse than "--". Staleness is a shape as well as a colour (ADR-017).
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `TwoSunsBatteryTest` (11 tests: 127, negative, 101, null, empty, one sample, hours-long gaps, out-of-order, stale, skew), `TwoSunsReadingsTest`, `TwoSunsCurveTest`. The simulator's history is synthetic (480 samples a minute apart, oldest-first, mostly dated in the future), so real cadence, order and sample count are unknown until the probe log.
**Reversed by.** Probe Q6 and Q7: fewer than 20 samples in 24 hours means 1-hour buckets ([`plan.md`](archive/plan.md) phase 1).
**Amended 2026-10-01** (ADR-020, the Free + Pro ladder, accepted 2026-10-04): in Free only the Complication path exists (no `SensorHistory` permission, no curve, no stale state): see ADR-021 (Body Battery in Free). Pro is unchanged.


## ADR-016: Rows follow font heights; drop order

**Decision.** Everything is a share of D, the shorter screen side. Each row takes the largest font up to a height cap (date 60 permille of D, time 260, value 80, sun line 75; the curve band at least 110), then the rows are stacked from those measured heights (Days To Go ADR-012), never from fractions. The stack may use 800 permille of the content circle's height. When it is too tall, optional rows drop in this order: **date, then curve, then sun line**. The time and the Body Battery value never drop. A curve that has less than 180 permille of D on its chord is dropped too, and the glyph and value stay. Rectangular screens are centred and sized by D. Ring and content circle are sized so text never touches the ring.
**Why.** System fonts do not scale with the screen; fractional bands overlapped on small screens in Days To Go.
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `everyStateFitsThisDisplay` (fails on a text cut with "...", an overlap, a dropped row or a stack taller than the span), `frameDropsRowsInOrder`, `framesDropRowsInOrderWhenTheScreenIsTiny`, `bigScreensKeepEveryRow`. The rectangles were not looked at by eye.
**Reversed by.** The owner's look approval changing the row set; a screen where the time or value cannot fit.
**Amended 2026-09-27** (owner, real FR965: "the time should be bigger... plenty of space"): time cap raised 230→260 permille of D. Proportional like every cap here, so it scales the same way on all 69 products; a screen too small for it already falls back through `TIME_FONTS`. **Not yet re-confirmed by a fit sweep or a new device photo** — do before the submit gate closes.

**Amended 2026-10-05 (ADR-028, the rectangle track).** On a rectangle the rows are fitted to the rounded box inside the track instead of the round chord, may use the box's whole height, are spread with equal gaps (`TwoSunsRectSpread`), and the time grows into what the box leaves (`TwoSunsRectFit`); the drop order is unchanged. The rectangles have since been looked at in simulator screenshots (not on a wrist).

## ADR-017: Ring, curve and glyph encodings

**Decision.** Ring: a night track all the way round (`#5555AA`, 3.3:1 against black); civil twilight `#AAAAFF`; daylight still to come in the accent, daylight already gone in the dimmed accent (each `FF` channel to `AA`, nudged one further step when that would land on `MUTED` — amended 2026-09-27, below); golden hour `#FF5500` only when the setting is on and a place is known; white ticks at sunrise and sunset; the sun marker at now, white with a black halo (the halo now wider than the dot itself — amended 2026-09-27, below), **solid when the sun is up, an outline when it is not**. No place or no data: a plain track and no marker. Midnight sun: fully lit. Polar night: dim, with twilight if it can be calculated. Curve: a fill (`#5555AA` fresh, `#555555` stale), a white line (muted when stale), the newest point a solid accent dot (fresh) or an outline dot (stale). Glyph: a level pill, not battery-shaped (no nub — amended 2026-09-27, owner feedback on a real-device photo: a battery-shaped glyph reads as watch battery on a Garmin face regardless of fill colour), a plain rounded bar filled left to right to the level in the accent, square-edged fill not rounded to match the outline (amended 2026-09-27, below), hollow when stale or when the value is "--". So stale is a shape as well as a colour, and a state is never colour alone.
**Why.** In bright sun the dim tracks must still read: the night track is at least 3:1 against black, and every dimmed accent is at least 4.6:1 (the unit test `dimPartsStayReadable` asserts at least 3:1 for the night track and for the dimmed form of all six). One more colour is dimmer: the stale curve fill is `#555555`, 2.8:1, awake-only, carried by shape and words rather than contrast.
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `TwoSunsRingPlanTest`, `TwoSunsCurveTest`, palette contrast test, `dimNeverEqualsMuted` (new). Contrast ratios are computed from the hex values, not measured on a screen. **The look has not been approved by the owner and no screenshot of the face exists.**
**Reversed by.** The owner's look decision, or a bright-sun check on a MIP watch.
**Amended 2026-09-27** (`watch-design-reviewer`, `watch-design-kit`), three fixes:
1. Winter (`#FFFFFF`) is the one accent made only of `AA`/`FF` channels, so the naive dim rule landed it on `#AAAAAA` — bit-identical to `MUTED`, the "no data" grey. That made a low winter-accent reading and no reading indistinguishable, the exact collision this ADR's own contrast reasoning depends on staying distinct. `TwoSunsPalette.dim()` now nudges the red channel one further step (`0x55AAAA`) whenever the naive result would equal `MUTED`; every other accent is unaffected.
2. The current-point dot's black "halo" was drawn at the same radius as the dot itself, so it left no visible margin — with the fresh-state line also drawn in `TEXT` (white), the winter accent's dot was white-on-white, invisible. The halo now draws at `dot + pen()`, wider than the dot.
3. Caught from a real FR965 **simulator** screenshot (owner, 2026-09-27 — corrected 2026-09-28: this was previously misstated as a real-device screenshot; the matching capture on file, `listing/screenshots.md`, says "Owner's FR965 simulator"): the glyph's fill rounded its own leading edge once wide enough, which at a mid-level reading (e.g. 59%) looked like a toggle-switch thumb floating in a track, not a level. The fill's leading (right, moving) edge is now a plain square rectangle; only the outline pill is rounded.

**Amended 2026-09-28** (code review, defensive): the trailing (left, anchored) edge of the glyph fill sits at the same x for every reading — coincident with the track's own left cap — so rounding it can't reproduce the "floating thumb" look point 3 above fixed; only the moving edge did that. Left square, its flat corners could in principle sit fractionally outside the track's true curved boundary there, for any nonzero reading (a geometry check: this only actually shows up once the cap radius exceeds roughly 3.4× the pen width, which the current constants keep marginal across the 69-product set, not confirmed on a real screenshot). `TwoSunsCurve.drawGlyph` now rounds the trailing edge defensively, with a radius capped so it can never exceed half the fill's own width or height — so it silently does nothing at very low fill (radius clamps toward 0, same as before) and can't recreate the amended-3 defect at any fill level, since the leading edge's shape is untouched. All 124 existing tests still pass unchanged (`curveAndGlyphDrawWithoutError` exercises this path); no new pixel-geometry test was added, matching this file's own existing testing limit ("Tests do not check pixels," this ADR's own Evidence line above).

## ADR-018: List settings only

**Decision.** Five settings, all lists: Accent colour (sky default, mint, autumn, violet, pink, winter — amended 2026-09-27: sky replaced amber as the default, and amber/white were renamed autumn/winter, same hex values, after the owner read amber as a "low" warning on a real-device photo of a normal Body Battery value; see ADR-017), Ring orientation (noon at the top default, midnight at the top), Golden hour (off), Energy curve (on), Date (on). Property keys `Accent`, `Orientation`, `Golden`, `Curve`, `Date` never change once shipped. Properties only; no `date` or `numeric` settings. Values are validated and fall back to the defaults, so the face works with all defaults if the phone round trip fails. Generated by `tools/gen_settings.py`.
**Why.** Days To Go ADR-003: Garmin Connect's date picker and numeric limits failed in rival faces; a list has nothing to validate.
**Evidence.** Simulator test: `TwoSunsSettingsTest`; `tools/gen_settings.py --check` verifies the generated files and the `KEY_*` constants (a Monkey C test cannot see a changed default in `properties.xml`, because the simulator keeps the last saved settings). Not tested on a phone or in Garmin Connect.
**Reversed by.** A phone test showing a list setting failing to save.
**Amended 2026-10-01** (ADR-020, the Free + Pro ladder, accepted 2026-10-04): Pro has all five settings; Free has Accent only (ids 0 to 5, the same six colours). Orientation, Golden hour, Energy curve and Date are Pro only; the Free properties file defines `Accent` alone.


## ADR-019: On-watch Customize (`getSettingsView`)

**Decision.** `TwoSunsApp.getSettingsView()` returns a `Menu2`/`Menu2InputDelegate` pair (`TwoSunsSettingsMenu`/`Delegate`, `source/settings/`) reaching the same five settings and the same Properties keys as `settings.xml`. Accent and Orientation push a second `Menu2` (`TwoSunsListMenu`/`Delegate`, one reusable pair, item id = the value to store); Golden, Curve and Date are `ToggleMenuItem`s that write on select. Same rule as Days To Go ADR-005: whichever route was used last wins, no merge. Like Days To Go, `getSettingsView` is wrapped in a try/catch that returns `null` on failure, so a settings-menu bug can never take the face down.
**Why.** Found 2026-09-27, on the owner's FR965: a sideloaded (non-Store) app gets no settings route at all from the phone's Garmin Connect app, which only renders a settings page for Store-installed apps. Without `getSettingsView`, none of the five settings were reachable during development. Days To Go already carries this exact pattern (ADR-005); Two Suns had only `settings.xml`, reversing spec.md's original "not part of v1" call.
**Evidence.** Compiles clean (`-w --typecheck 3`), zero warnings; simulator tests unaffected (no test yet exercises the menu itself — Menu2 interaction is not covered by `TwoSunsSettingsTest`, which only checks the Properties/defaults layer). Confirmed on the owner's FR965, 2026-09-27: Customize appears next to Apply, and all five settings round-trip.
**Reversed by.** A device check showing Customize missing, wrong values after a round trip, or a crash opening the menu.
**Amended 2026-10-01** (ADR-020, the Free + Pro ladder, accepted 2026-10-04): the Free menu has the Accent item only; Pro's has the five, unchanged. Free never writes a Pro key (`setValue` of a key the properties file lacks throws).

## ADR-021: Body Battery in Free

**Status: Accepted (Active) 2026-10-04: the owner approved the ladder 2026-10-04, with ADR-020 (the Free + Pro ladder); the owner also decided that Free keeps `--` for a missing Body Battery number (2026-10-04).** Written 2026-10-01 as Proposed. Plan WP5 step 4 asked what Free shows when the Body Battery complication is null, because Free has no history and so the "stale reading" state of ADR-015 (Body Battery data rules; newest sample older than 60 minutes) cannot exist.

**Decision.** Free's Body Battery is Garmin's own number from `Complications` BODY_BATTERY, and nothing else; Pro's path is unchanged.
- **A valid number** (0 to 100) is the value, in the accent and dimmed below 30 as in Pro (ADR-008, no verdicts on Body Battery), and fills the level pill.
- **Null, not a number, or outside 0 to 100** (`TwoSunsBattery.isValidValue`: 127 means not worn): the value is `--` and the pill is hollow. This is the same display Pro already gives a watch with no history API and no valid complication number (ADR-015, Body Battery data rules), and the form this project's house rule uses for a value the watch does not have ("No place yet", "No sun data", `--`: `../CLAUDE.md`). It is never blank, and it carries no verdict, no mood and no advice. The state is also a shape (a hollow pill), not colour alone (ADR-017, ring, curve and glyph encodings).
- **There is no stale state in Free.** `Complications.Complication` has no timestamp (SDK 9.2.0, `Toybox/Complications/Complication.html`: `complicationId`, `longLabel`, `ranges`, `shortLabel`, `unit`, `value`), so the age of the number cannot be known and the face does not claim it: no muting, no "old" wording, no hollow dot. The number is as current as Garmin's complication; the listing and site must say "Garmin's own number", never "live" or "accurate" (`release-contract.md`).
- **Colour of `--`:** the value is drawn in the accent, not MUTED, and the pill outline is white (`TwoSunsView.drawBand`: MUTED only when `batteryStale`; `freeBatteryIsTheComplicationOnly` asserts `batteryAccent == accent`). That is the existing Pro behaviour for a history with no valid sample, left unchanged: `TwoSunsPalette` calls MUTED "the no-data grey" in a comment, but the view uses it for stale only. Making `--` MUTED in both tiers is a one-line owner choice (it would not collide with a low reading, which dims the accent, but would collide with stale in Pro, which has no `--`).
- **The simulator invents the number.** Its Body Battery complication returns a different value on each read (96, then 75, seen 2026-10-01 in `secondReadIsTheSame`); Free's number is Garmin's live complication, so that test is split in two twins (`assertSameBattery`: Pro compares the history-backed text, Free only checks that both reads gave a value). Not a Free defect.
- **No curve, no history call, no new string.** The curve is Pro. `TwoSunsSources.batteryCurve` is the `(:free)` twin that returns null; the shared `TwoSunsReadings.fillBattery` already handles a null curve.
- Tests: `freeBatteryIsTheComplicationOnly` (number, `--`, 127, -1 and 101, never stale, never a curve, never a level for `--`), `freeStateHasNoProState`, and the shared `batteryFallsBackToTheComplication`. Compiled for the Free jungle, **not yet run**.

**Why.** The words rule ("every failure has a sentence, never a blank", ADR-013, sky states) was written for the sun sentence, which has a row of its own. The Body Battery band has room for a number and a pill only: `--` plus a hollow pill is what the project already uses there, and a worded value ("No data") in the value slot would need a font that fits the band, a measured-fit check on 15 languages and 14 machine-drafted translations, which the owner has not agreed to (unreviewed translations are an owner decision).
**Alternatives.** A short word in the value slot ("No data", fitted by `TwoSunsDraw`): rejected for now, for the translation cost above; the owner may choose it. A sentence on the sun line when the number is missing: rejected, it would mix two sources in one row. Substituting a stale number: no source for one.
**Evidence.** Simulator test, written and compiled, **not run**; the SDK page above. No device has shown what Garmin's complication returns on a watch that never had a reading.
**Reversed by.** The owner choosing a worded empty state; Garmin adding a timestamp to `Complication`; a device run showing the complication is null for hours on a worn watch (then "--" would be the wrong thing to show for long).

**Amended 2026-10-05 (ADR-028, the rectangle track).** On a rectangle `--` is drawn muted, like the hollow bolt beside it; round screens still draw it in the accent. Whether round should follow is an owner question for the rectangle look approval.

## ADR-027: Always-on text is a dim grey, `#5C5C5C`

**Status: Accepted 2026-10-04 (owner, ROADMAP 10.26).** Changes the always-on text colour of ADR-007 (burn-in protection; amended 2026-09-27 to `#5555AA`); the rest of ADR-007 stands.

**Decision.** `TwoSunsPalette.SLEEP_TEXT` is `#5C5C5C`, not `#5555AA`. It is used only by the AMOLED sleep frame (`TwoSunsSleep`, and the dimmed time in `TwoSunsView`); the sky night track stays `#5555AA` (the energy-curve fill, also `#5555AA` then, was removed 2026-10-05, ROADMAP 13.15), and the Instinct palette is unchanged (white).

**Why.** Garmin's watch-face guidance for always-on says "Avoid using much white or blue. Consider using light gray instead" (finding in `DESIGN.md`, "Always-on text colour against Garmin's avoid much white or blue"; `../../reports/Garmin policies and design guidelines.md`). The advice has no stated reason, but the owner chose to follow it rather than wait for the wrist check. `#5C5C5C` is the dimmest neutral grey that keeps this project's own 3:1 bar against black: 3.14:1 (`#555555`, the 64-colour grey, is 2.8:1 and was already rejected in the 2026-09-27 amendment). Relative luminance 0.107 against 0.113 for `#5555AA`, so the Venu 2 and later luminance budget (under 10% of the screen) is slightly better, and the original Venu's lit-pixel budget is untouched (the same glyphs on the same grid, at the same step). It is not one of the 64-colour values; the sleep frame only runs on AMOLED, which renders any value.

**Evidence.** Computed from the hex value (WCAG formula, the same one `ringContrast` in the tests uses); unit test `dimPartsStayReadable` now also asserts the always-on text is at least 3:1. Unit suite and screen-fit tests (`everyStateFitsThisDisplay`, `alwaysOnFrameFitsAtEveryDrift`) run in the container on fr965, fr255s, epix2 and instincte40mm for both tiers, simulator only (results in `status.md`). **Since measured in the simulator only:** the simulator's 24-hour heat map (File > View Screen Heat Map, always-on) reports no burn-in detected, peak luminance 1.03% (Pro) and 1.27% (Free) on `venusq2`, 1.57% (Pro and Free) on `venux1` (2026-10-07, final build) (first run 2026-10-05, ADR-028, the rectangle track; `docs/compatibility.md`) and 1.09% on `fr965` (2026-10-05, `../docker/SIMULATOR.md`); simulator only, the other round sizes not run, nothing on a wrist. **Not measured:** ghosting, legibility on a wrist in daylight or at night (ROADMAP 3.1). The simulator is not device proof; a grey of 3.1:1 on a real AMOLED outdoors is a look-approval question for the owner.

**Reversed by.** The always-on night or the heat map (ROADMAP 3.1) showing the text unreadable or the frame over Garmin's limit: one constant, `SLEEP_TEXT`.

## ADR-028: Rectangles: the sky ring follows the screen

**Status: Proposed 2026-10-05, built, simulator only.** The owner asked for square designs on square watches (studio rule for rectangles, 2026-10-05) and chose "build first, then approve the real simulator screenshots before any upload". Changes the rectangle line of ADR-009 (device set: the rectangles "keep the round design centred"), the rectangle part of ADR-016 (rows follow font heights), ADR-023's (watch battery row) "never moves another row" on rectangles, ADR-025's (Free's Body Battery number is a size larger) rule on rectangles, ADR-007's (always-on) font list on rectangles and ADR-021's (Body Battery in Free) accent `--` on rectangles; each carries an amendment note; ADR-017 (ring, curve and glyph encodings) and ADR-008 (no verdicts on Body Battery) stand unchanged: every meaning and colour is the round face's, with two rectangle-only differences, the muted `--` and the hidden lone-dot curve (decision 4).

**Decision.** On `venusq2`, `venusq2m` and `venux1` (`System.SCREEN_SHAPE_RECTANGLE`; round and Instinct screens never build it):
1. The sky ring is a rounded-rectangle track (`TwoSunsTrack`), with the round ring's width, inset from the glass as the round ring is from the bezel (half the width plus the gap) or further, so the sun marker and its black halo stay on the glass (`TwoSunsLayout.trackInsetFor`: 9 px on the Venu Sq 2, 11 px on the Venu X1; the round ring's bezel hides that overhang, a rectangle's glass does not), corner radius 150 permille of D. It starts at top centre and runs clockwise; a stretch of the day is the same share of the track's length (straight runs plus quarter-circle corners), so the 24 hours are equal time per length, noon (or midnight, setting Orientation) at top centre. Every layer, the ticks (perpendicular to the track) and the sun marker sit on that path.
2. Rows fit the rounded box inside the track (corners concentric with it) instead of the round chord, and the stack may use the box's whole height.
3. Once every row has its place, the time takes the largest number font that still fits the box by height and by the width of "00:00" (`TwoSunsRectFit`, `FONT_NUMBER_THAI_HOT` first). No row is dropped for it: a size that would push the weather row off its chord is skipped for the next smaller one. The size is planned for the Weather setting, not for the data: while Weather is on, the full row's height is kept in the plan even when there is no data, so the time does not jump when data comes and goes (coordinator's pick 2026-10-06, after the second design review). In Free the Body Battery number grows with the time (`FONT_LARGE`, `FONT_MEDIUM` or `FONT_SMALL`, no taller than 45% of the time font) so the energy reading stays clearly second. The rows are spread over the box with equal gaps between the rows' boxes, margins included (the time's box by its digits; `TwoSunsRectSpread`; the time font's empty band under its baseline and an equal band above are not counted as gap; font boxes never overlap and stay in the box; places never depend on the sentence's wording); a time size only fits when that spread still holds, so growth never eats the spacing, and only a stack that cannot be spread keeps the centred round stack. While the Battery setting is on, Pro keeps a strip for the watch battery row at the top of the box (the row and one gap, no twin below), but only when the row then fits and draws; with the setting off (the default), or where the row would not draw anyway, the time takes that room, so a setting that shows nothing never costs the time a size. So on a rectangle the row can step the time down a size, an exception to ADR-023's (watch battery row) "never moves the stack", which round screens keep. Pro's Body Battery number may also take one step up (`FONT_SMALL` or `FONT_TINY`, up to 45% of the time font; it shares the band with the curve). The always-on time is two steps below the grown awake time, in a list that continues past `FONT_NUMBER_MILD`, so it is always smaller than awake.
4. The Body Battery band spans the box (no width cap on the curve); the hollow bolt's outline stroke scales with the bolt (a twentieth of its height when that is more than the round pen, `TwoSunsCurve.outlinePen`), since the bolt grows with the number; `--` is drawn muted like the hollow bolt beside it (round still draws it in the accent: whether round should follow is an owner question); and a curve with no two neighbouring samples is not drawn there: a lone dot at the far end of a box-wide band read as floating in the 2026-10-05 review screenshots ("59" and the blue dot).

**Why.** The round ring inscribed in a rectangle left the corners and the top and bottom bands empty and the Body Battery number and curve dot floating in the circle (review screenshots `device-test/rect-review/before/`). A track along the glass is the round ring's sibling: same thickness, same inset, same encoding by share; mapping time by length (not by angle from the centre) keeps an hour the same length everywhere on the track, so the corners do not stretch or squeeze time. The corner radius is one proportion so both sizes look alike, and large enough to clear the Venu X1's rounded glass (68 px at 448 px wide, measured off the alpha mask of the SDK's `venux1/device.png`; the Venu Sq 2's is about 10 px, `venusq2.png`). HeroFace ADR-005 (the ring as a frame) solved the same problem for its ring; ideas only, no code is shared.

**Evidence.** Simulator only (container, SDK 9.2.0): new tests `trackMapsATimeToTheRightPoint`, `trackStaysOnTheDisplay` (both sizes, every 5 minutes, the outer edge inside the display and the measured glass corner), `sunMarkerStaysOnTheGlass` (both sizes, every minute, the marker and its halo inside the measured glass), `trackBoxFollowsItsCorners`, `layoutBuildsTheTrackOnlyOnARectangle`, `rectangleHidesACurveWithNoLine`, `rectangleGrowthKeepsTheWeatherRow`, `rectangleFreeNumberStaysSecond`, `rectangleBatteryCostsTheTimeOnlyWhenDrawn`, `rectangleAlwaysOnTimeIsSmaller`, `rectangleSpreadGapsAreEven`, `rectangleRowsDoNotDependOnTheWording`; the screen-fit tests pass on `venusq2` and `venux1` in both tiers; screenshots on both sizes (`device-test/rect-review/after/`): both tiers in the morning, day, evening and night, with the marker on the corners, in the empty and low states and always-on; Pro also with its default settings, with the battery row on, midnight at the top, a stale curve, and a sunrise tick with golden hour on a corner (Free has no stale state, ADR-021); the simulator's 24-hour heat map, always-on: no burn-in detected, peak luminance 1.03% (Pro) and 1.27% (Free) on `venusq2`, 1.57% (Pro and Free) on `venux1` (2026-10-07, final build). The round sizes were not re-measured for this ADR (`fr965` 1.09% on 2026-10-05, `../docker/SIMULATOR.md`). Round (`fr965`, `fr255s`) and Instinct (`instincte40mm`) suites pass unchanged. The sunrise tick and the golden-hour edge on a corner were shot with sun times moved onto the top-left corner (sunrise 09:05), on both sizes. Runs: the suites 2026-10-07 (Pro 165, Free 76 on `venusq2`, `venux1`, `fr965`, `fr255s`; 157 and 69 on `instincte40mm`), the 15-language fit on both rectangles and both tiers 2026-10-06/07. **Not seen:** any rectangle on a wrist; the real glass margins; Venu Sq 2 Music (same screen as Sq 2, compile only).

**Reversed by.** The owner rejecting the look in the screenshot review, or a wrist showing the track clipped by the glass (then the corner proportion, `TwoSunsTrack.CORNER_PERMILLE`, or the inset changes); removing `TwoSunsLayout._track` restores the round design on rectangles.
