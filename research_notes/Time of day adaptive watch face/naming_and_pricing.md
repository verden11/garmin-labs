# Time-of-day adaptive contextual watch face/widget — Naming and Pricing

Research snapshot: 2026-09-27. Name-collision checks used general web search (Google-indexed results, which reliably surface individual `apps.garmin.com` listing pages when they exist — confirmed by directly finding the "DayShift" Connect IQ listing this way) plus targeted Connect IQ store page fetches. No trademark-register search (e.g. USPTO/EUIPO) was run — "no collision found" below means no product, app, or trademark use turned up in general web and store search, not a cleared legal trademark search.

## Brainstorm 5-10 candidate short app names in this studio's style, and report any collision found for each

### Takeaway
Of 12 candidate names tested, 6 came back with no product, app, or trademark use found anywhere (HeroDay, DayArc, Daybeat, DayCue, Daybreaker, Guideday); 6 collided with an existing app or, in one case, an existing Connect IQ store listing (Dayline, DayPulse, Daymark, Daywise, Dayrise, DayShift). **DayShift is a direct in-store collision — a existing free Connect IQ watch face by "johnnyw2" already uses that exact name** — and must be dropped outright. The cleanest style fits among the collision-free set are **HeroDay** (matches the studio's existing "Hero-" prefix family used by HeroSet/HeroFace) and **DayArc** (evokes the day's rhythm/arc the way "TwoSuns" evokes two suns, without being descriptive).

### Cited Findings
- **DayShift** is an existing, live Connect IQ Store watch face by developer "johnnyw2" (free, ~100+ downloads, 3.2★) — [DayShift | Connect IQ Store](https://apps.garmin.com/pl-PL/apps/34971426-f5ee-4e06-acf1-2ad6396f4f17). Direct in-store name collision; reject.
- **Dayline** collides heavily outside Garmin: a "Dayline Watch Face" already exists for Wear OS ([Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.MOONApps.daylinewatchface)), plus at least four unrelated "Dayline"/"DayLine" apps (a 5-year diary, a daily planner, a habit/reflection journal) across iOS and Android — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dayline-tasks-calendar/id6751211696), [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.aporia.dayline&hl=en). Reject — the collision is specifically strong because a Wear-adjacent watch face already carries the name.
- **DayPulse** is an existing task-management app (Apprizon LLC, iOS since October 2022; also on Android) with the identical name — [AppBrain](https://www.appbrain.com/appstore/daypulse/ios-955035393). Reject.
- **Daymark** collides with at least five unrelated apps: a daily journal, a countdown app, a habit tracker, a mood tracker, and "DayMark Wealth Partners" (a financial-advisory app) — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/daymark-daily-journal/id6760376611), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/daymark-date-countdown/id6751110336). Reject.
- **Daywise** is an existing, established habit-tracking app and, separately, an existing Android notification-batching app of the same name — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.appsur.daywise&hl=en_IN). Reject.
- **Dayrise** is an existing habit-tracker app with its own marketing site — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/habit-tracker-dayrise/id6749515833), [drise.app](https://drise.app/). Reject.
- **HeroDay** — no product, app, watch face, or trademark use found in general web search or in Connect IQ-specific search. Fits the studio's existing "Hero-" naming family (HeroSet, HeroFace) directly.
- **DayArc** — no product, app, watch face, or trademark use found in general web search or in Connect IQ-specific search.
- **Daybeat** — no exact-name collision found; nearest neighbours are the unrelated "DayBand" fitness tracker and a "Beat Watch Face" (LED-style face), neither an exact match — [App Store: DayBand](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/dayband/id1461078333).
- **DayCue** — no exact-name collision found; nearest neighbours are "WatchCue" (a where-to-watch-movies app, unrelated domain) and "Day One" (a journaling app), neither an exact match.
- **Daybreaker** — no product, app, watch face, or trademark use found in general web search.
- **Guideday** — no product, app, watch face, or trademark use found in general web search; weakest fit stylistically (reads generic/corporate rather than evocative, unlike TwoSuns/DaysToGo).
- General trademark-risk context: a Swiss court has ruled that certain watch-face apps violate **Swatch's trademark rights** over watch-face branding — a live precedent that watch-face naming carries real trademark exposure beyond simple app-name collision — [swissinfo.ch](https://www.swissinfo.ch/eng/business/watch-face-apps-violate-swatch-trademark-rights-court-rules/49063424). None of the candidates above use Swatch- or Swiss-watch-brand-adjacent terms, but this precedent supports the owner doing a real trademark search (as flagged, unresolved, in the prior TwoSuns naming research) before committing to any final name, not just repeating the same web/store search done here.

### Inferences
- The studio's existing names (HeroSet, HeroFace, DaysToGo, TwoSuns) are either "Hero-" prefixed compounds or short evocative phrases that say nothing literal about the feature set. HeroDay fits the first pattern exactly; DayArc, Daybeat, DayCue, and Daybreaker fit the second pattern (evocative of a day's shape/rhythm without describing "adaptive guidance" literally), consistent with TwoSuns not describing "sun ring plus Body Battery curve."
- Names built on common self-improvement/productivity vocabulary ("-wise," "-line," "-mark," "-rise," "-pulse," "-shift" combined with "Day") are heavily colonized by journaling, habit-tracking, and productivity apps — six of six candidates in that lexical family collided. Names built on less habit-tracker-coded vocabulary ("-arc," "-beat," "-cue," "-breaker," "Hero-") came back clean across the same search method, suggesting the safer naming space for this product sits outside the "daily productivity app" word-cloud entirely.
- Because DayShift is an existing free Connect IQ watch face with a modest download count (100+) and a low rating (3.2★), it is a weak but real incumbent — not a wall against a differently-styled app of the same category, but the exact-name collision is disqualifying regardless of the incumbent's own low traction.

### Gaps
- No formal trademark-register search (USPTO, EUIPO, or equivalent) was performed for any candidate — this matches the caveat already recorded in the prior TwoSuns naming research (`research_notes/Body Battery and sun face research/naming.md`) and remains open for whichever name is chosen.
- Search coverage of non-English app stores and non-English trademark use was not attempted; a name clean in English-language search could still collide in another market's storefront.
- General web search cannot browse Garmin's Connect IQ store search-by-keyword UI directly (WebFetch could not reach an API endpoint independent of a rendered listing page), so absence of a hit is inferred from Google's indexing of individual `apps.garmin.com/*/apps/{uuid}` pages rather than from querying Garmin's own search index directly. The DayShift hit is direct evidence this indexing does surface real Connect IQ listings, which raises confidence in the other candidates' "no collision found," but it is not the same as running the query inside Garmin's own store search box.

## Survey free/paid pricing patterns for comparable multi-context Connect IQ faces/widgets

### Takeaway
Two prior local reports already established the core pattern and should not be re-derived: paid Connect IQ watch faces are normal (51 of the top-120 most-popular faces carry a native Garmin price, band €2.49–€5.99) and configurability is the single strongest correlate of success in the top-100 popular faces (declared by 80% of top descriptions) — see `reports/Garmin watch face market gap.md`. Separately, paid "works for polish and breadth, not for a narrow single-idea face" — Circles 2 (a broad, customisable dashboard) is the one paid success story at 3.49€/10,000+ downloads/4.8★/1,716 reviews among Body-Battery-or-sun-themed faces, while every paid *narrow* single-idea face in that survey tops out at 2 examples reaching 1,000 downloads — see `reports/Body Battery and sun face research.md`. New evidence gathered here for *multi-context, time-of-day-adaptive* apps specifically (as opposed to sun/Body-Battery apps) reinforces the same pattern and adds one strong direct precedent: **M2**, a free, highly configurable Connect IQ watch face that lets users define separate display "profiles" keyed to sunrise/sunset/dawn/dusk-relative time windows — i.e. exactly the "morning mode vs. evening mode" mechanic this product proposes — has 50,000+ downloads and a 4.9★ rating, entirely free.

### Cited Findings
- **M2** (Connect IQ watch face, developer mark.ai): free, 50,000+ downloads, 4.9★. Lets users design multiple display "profiles" and schedule them to load automatically at different times of day, with start/end times settable relative to sunrise/sunset/dawn/dusk (e.g. "SUNRISE-00:30") — the closest found precedent for a Connect IQ product that natively changes its displayed content by time of day — [Connect IQ Store](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/1b4e3210-8105-487b-9f3a-8d538e6fed55); [Garmin Forums showcase thread](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/showcase/213203/watchface-m2).
- **Periodic Reminder** (Connect IQ widget, developer Trudelta): free, 1,000+ downloads, 2.8★ — a single-purpose periodic-notification widget, the closest found analog to the "midday nudge" mechanic on its own, without other contextual features bundled in — [Connect IQ Store](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/31572290-9ef1-46fc-bb1b-245be3f5ee23).
- **Stress Meter** (Connect IQ widget, developer Trudelta): paid (exact price not retrievable from the fetched page, listed as a purchase rather than free), 1,000+ downloads, 4.8★ — a single-purpose stress-display widget that holds a high rating despite being paid and narrow, in contrast to Periodic Reminder's free-but-2.8★ result from the same developer — [Connect IQ Store](https://apps.garmin.com/apps/d88d7ef0-7430-4968-b5e7-b4668696ee45).
- **Stress and relaxation cycles** (Connect IQ widget): free, 1,000+ downloads, 3.0★ — another single-purpose stress-context widget with a mediocre rating — [Connect IQ Store](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/5c449a00-5643-4ab9-8b3c-715a19a0fc67).
- **SuperTask (Widget)** (Connect IQ widget, developer PedlarStudios): free, 1,000+ downloads, 2.3★ — a general task-management widget; low rating despite being free, consistent with the broader corpus finding that free alone does not buy a good rating. — [Connect IQ Store](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/9a0c6082-3216-4573-b800-58a21dba97c5).
- From the adjacent (non-Garmin) research already filed by a parallel researcher in this same folder (`competitor_landscape.md`), directly relevant to the morning "dress-for-weather" feature: **Weather Fit: What to Wear** (iOS/Apple Watch complication) is free with in-app purchases at **$3.99/month, $19.99/year, or $99.99 lifetime**, 4.6★ from 3.8K ratings — the only found named, priced example of "dress for weather" guidance on any wearable platform, and it uses a subscription/lifetime-tier model rather than a single Connect IQ-style flat price — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/weather-fit-what-to-wear/id1194408342).
- Same file: **Countdown!** (Connect IQ, event-countdown face) free, 4.1★, 100,000+ downloads; **GLANCE** (Connect IQ, unrelated to this project, clean/legible dashboard face) free, 4.9★, 1,000,000+ downloads — both free and both far larger than any paid narrow face found in either this survey or the prior Body Battery/sun survey — [Connect IQ Store: Countdown!](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/183a7d45-c05e-4964-ba16-8f238eab2ba6), [Connect IQ Store: GLANCE](https://apps.garmin.com/apps/07ae0f49-7240-4475-a229-4507e8035fae).

### Inferences
- Across every multi-context or configurable Connect IQ example found (M2, GLANCE, Countdown!, and the top-120 ranking already documented in `Garmin watch face market gap.md`), the highest download counts belong to **free** products; the one paid success story at real scale in either survey (Circles 2, from the prior Body Battery/sun report) is broad and customisable rather than narrow. This is now a repeated pattern across three independent surveys (general market-gap survey, Body-Battery/sun survey, this time-of-day survey), which raises confidence that **a genuinely multi-context, time-of-day-adaptive product is a stronger candidate for the "configurable, broad, defensibly free-or-low-priced" bucket than for a premium single price point**, echoing the already-recorded owner decision to price TwoSuns and Days To Go at the lowest paid tier rather than higher.
- The Stress Meter vs. Periodic Reminder contrast (same developer, one paid-and-4.8★, one free-and-2.8★) is a small-sample but clean illustration that **price is not what separates a well-received widget from a poorly-received one on Connect IQ; execution is** — directly consistent with the market-gap report's finding that ratings cluster high across the board and review volume/complaint-theme are the real discriminators, not price.
- No Connect IQ example was found that bundles all three of this product's proposed contexts (dress-for-weather, midday nudges, evening energy summary) in one listing, so there is no direct precedent to price against for the full concept — the closest available comparables are single-context widgets (Stress Meter, Periodic Reminder, Stress and relaxation cycles, all in the free–low-paid, 1,000+ download tier) and one multi-mode watch face (M2, free, 50,000+, time-of-day-profile mechanic but no health/stress/weather logic). This gap should be read together with the parallel `competitor_landscape.md` finding that no shipped product anywhere (Connect IQ, Wear OS, or Apple Watch OS-level) natively switches its own content by time of day the way this concept proposes — the pricing question is therefore being answered by analogy to adjacent categories, not to a true comparable.

### Gaps
- Exact price for Stress Meter could not be retrieved (the fetched store page rendered a payment indicator rather than a numeric price); a manual store visit or logged-in fetch would be needed to get the figure.
- Review *counts* (as opposed to star ratings) could not be retrieved for M2, Periodic Reminder, Stress Meter, Stress and relaxation cycles, or SuperTask from the fetched pages — only aggregate rating and download-bucket rendered. This matches the same store-page-fetch limitation already noted in the parallel `competitor_landscape.md` file in this folder ("Garmin's store page doesn't expose review text via fetch").
- Whether M2's time-of-day profile-switching feature drives its 50,000+ download count and 4.9★ rating, versus its general configurability/aesthetic quality, could not be determined — no review text was retrievable to attribute cause. Treat "M2 succeeds" as correlated with, not proven caused by, the time-of-day mechanic.
- No Connect IQ or wearable-platform example bundling dress-for-weather + event/break nudges + evening energy summary in one paid or free listing was found anywhere, so there is no direct price-band precedent for the full combined concept — only for its individual pieces (see Inferences).
