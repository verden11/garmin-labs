# Sun window app is buildable, but narrow

A Connect IQ "sun window" watch-app is **technically feasible on API 5.1+ watches and worth building only as a small, calm, no-numbers alternative to existing rivals**. Every input the design needs (hourly UV and cloud cover, a notification call, a background timer, step counts, a location) exists in the Connect IQ SDK, and solar elevation can be computed offline to better than 0.01 degrees. But three links in the nudge chain have never run on a watch: background reads of weather and activity data, whether the temporal event fires for a watch-app, and whether UV and cloud values are ever non-null. The market is small and no longer empty. The paid direct rival SunIQ shipped a "best and safest time to go outside" window screen with a reminder on 2026-10-02 ([SunIQ listing](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/f8ece1ea-dc33-4b01-8ca0-1d938262d891)), it has only 100+ downloads after eight months, and all four live Connect IQ apps with "Vitamin D" in the title sit at 1,000 downloads or fewer ([store API](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/f6aa38aa-ac02-4a99-8a9e-c0674f27f933?countryCode=US)). The science supports a sun-height rule but no single threshold: 45 degrees is the most conservative and easiest to explain, and it makes "none today" the normal state in Vilnius or London for about 7.5 months a year. My recommendation is a conditional go: spend one day on a device spike of the background chain before any design work, build the Free glance first, keep "vitamin D" out of the title, and treat the go/no-go against SunIQ as the owner's call. Name, price tier placement, description wording and the Free/Pro boundary are listed as owner decisions at the end.

## The data exists, but the nudge chain has never run on a watch

The raw inputs are better than expected. `Toybox.Weather` gives `uvIndex` (Float, "[0-10]") and `cloudCover` (Number, "[0-100%]") on both current conditions and hourly forecasts, introduced at API level 5.1.0; precipitation chance and a 54-value condition enum exist since 3.2.0; the daily forecast carries neither UV nor cloud ([CurrentConditions](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/Weather/CurrentConditions.html), [HourlyForecast](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/Weather/HourlyForecast.html)). No manifest permission is needed to read weather. The SDK's device files put **75 products at API 5.1 or newer, of which 66 are watches** (fēnix 7/8/9, FR165 through FR970, Venu 3/4, vívoactive 5/6, Instinct 3/E and more); Venu 2, FR945/745/245 and fēnix 6 are below the line ([FR965 device file](file:///Users/mbp/Library/Application%20Support/Garmin/ConnectIQ/Devices/fr965/compiler.json)). On the owner's FR965 the hourly list held 12 entries starting at the current hour, so "worse later" can only look about 12 hours ahead and never to tomorrow ([TwoSuns ADR-022, weather evidence on FR965](../TwoSuns/docs/decisions.md)). There is no sun-elevation API in the SDK, so the app must compute it from date, time and latitude/longitude; `Weather.getSunrise` needs a location anyway.

Three further choices follow from Garmin's own rules. A background `ServiceDelegate` may run at most every 5 minutes, must exit within 30 seconds, and cannot call `Attention` (so it cannot vibrate itself), but the compiler's scope model allows `Weather`, `ActivityMonitor`, `Position`, `Storage` and `Notifications` there ([Background API](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/Background.html)). `Notifications.showNotification` (API 5.1.0) needs the `Notifications` permission, which the SDK's own permission map allows for watch-apps and omits for the `widget` type, and Garmin's sample calls it from a temporal event in a watch-app ([Notifications API](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/Notifications.html)). So the app type is **watch-app with a glance**, not widget. There is no "time since last move" field anywhere in `ActivityMonitor.Info`; inactivity has to be inferred from step snapshots stored between background runs, with `moveBarLevel` (0 to 5) as an undocumented cross-check ([ActivityMonitor.Info](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/api-docs/Toybox/ActivityMonitor/Info.html)). Location is the awkward part: on the FR965 `Activity.currentLocation` and the Weather observation location stayed null, and only `Position.getInfo()` with the `Positioning` permission returned a cached fix ([TwoSuns ADR-005, location probes on FR965](../TwoSuns/docs/decisions.md)).

Grading matters here, because the notes separate compile-time scope data from watch behaviour and so must this report. Simulator results are not device proof.

## A 45 degree sun-height rule is a convention, not a threshold of nature

The sources split into three families, and none is authoritative. The "shadow shorter than you are tall" rule is an elevation of at least 45 degrees, attributed to MacLaughlin 1982 and Webb 2011 in a 2024 paper ([Kallioglu 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10861575/)). The UV Index 3 line is Cancer Council Australia's and WHO's point at which sun protection starts, and where "a few minutes outdoors" can suffice for most people ([Cancer Council](https://www.cancer.org.au/preventing-cancer/sun-protection/vitamin-d/), [WHO guide](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/94a908ec-14ee-4bb4-84eb-ace483c9eb51/content)). Lower cut-offs of about 15 to 30 degrees mark where any UVB reaches the ground and are cited only secondhand ([Leal and Leder 2024](https://publicacoes.amigosdanatureza.org.br/index.php/gerenciamento_de_cidades/article/download/4190/4946/10686)). Using Allaart's clear-sky fit ([KNMI paper](https://www.temis.nl/uvradiation/product/papers/200411105metappl.pdf)), the research computed that clear-sky UVI 3 falls at about **37 degrees at 300 DU ozone, and 33 to 44 degrees across 250 to 400 DU**. That is my colleague's reading of an equation whose printed layout was garbled, so treat it as an estimate with roughly 20 percent model error.

The empirical anchor is Webb's 1988 finding that cloudless days produced no previtamin D3 in Boston from November to February and in Edmonton from October to March; the Boston result is verified in [Engelsen 2010](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3257661/) and [Wacker and Holick 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3897598/), and the UK's expert committee says UVB is sufficient year-round only below 37 degrees North ([SACN 2016](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/5a804e36ed915d74e622dafa/SACN_Vitamin_D_and_Health_report.pdf)). Calibrating against those observations, the notes place the on/off point between roughly 36 and 45 degrees for northern spring. Two things push toward the 45 degree end. Vitamin D-weighted UV falls off faster with low sun than sunburn UV does (winter vitamin D UV is about 5 percent of summer, versus about 10 percent for sunburn UV) ([NIWA](https://niwa.co.nz/water-atmosphere/vol17-no1-march-2009/balancing-risks-and-benefits-uv-radiation)), so UVI 3 is generous for synthesis specifically. And the iPhone "Vitamin D Window Widget" already uses the same convention, "above 45 degrees for optimal, above 30 for partial" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6759223422), summarised by a fetch model, not verbatim). I therefore propose **one documented constant, 45 degrees, described to users as a display rule ("the window opens when the sun is above 45 degrees"), never as a biological fact**.

Cloud is the largest uncertainty and should only demote, never refine. The US National Weather Service factors are clear 100 percent, scattered 89, broken 73 and overcast 31 percent of clear-sky UV ([EPA](https://19january2017snapshot.epa.gov/sunsafety/calculating-uv-index-0_.html)); WHO notes up to 80 percent of UV passes light cloud ([WHO guide](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/94a908ec-14ee-4bb4-84eb-ace483c9eb51/content)); and cloud contributes over 50 percent uncertainty to a practical UV forecast ([Allaart 2004](https://www.temis.nl/uvradiation/product/papers/200411105metappl.pdf)). Whether Garmin's `uvIndex` is cloud-adjusted, or capped at its documented 10, is not stated anywhere the research could read. The rule is therefore: elevation is the deterministic backbone; if hourly `uvIndex` is non-null and below 3, or `cloudCover` is in the overcast band, the window is demoted. The percent cut for "overcast" is my assumption from eighths-of-sky categories, not a sourced number, and must be tuned.

The honest cost of 45 degrees is a long closed season. From the fixtures scan of every day of 2026, the noon sun reaches 45 degrees only on **Apr 15 to Aug 27 in Vilnius (135 days), Apr 6 to Sep 5 in London (153), Feb 18 to Oct 22 in Phoenix (247), May 16 to Jul 26 in Reykjavik (72), and Jan 1 to Apr 18 plus Aug 25 to Dec 31 in Sydney (237)**; Singapore is open all year ([fixtures](../research_notes/Vitamin%20D%20window/solar_elevation_fixtures.md)). For northern users the third state, "none today", will show most of the year, which is physically honest and a retention problem. The UVI 3 line is also double-edged: it is simultaneously "enough UV to work with" for vitamin D and "start protecting" for WHO and Cancer Council, and WHO warns that burn-time framings invite longer exposure. That is the main reason the product should describe the sun, not the skin ([WHO guide](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/94a908ec-14ee-4bb4-84eb-ace483c9eb51/content)).

## The window idea is already claimed, and the niche is small

SunIQ ("Vitamin D Tracker, UV & Sunburn Alerts", developer 3303, Lviv) is the direct rival: paid, first released 2026-02-10, 4.3 stars from 10 reviews, v2.5 on 2026-10-02 with a daily screen colouring hours green ("optimal and safest"), red ("dangerous UV levels") or grey, plus a reminder in its "CRITICAL" zone. Its claims are heavy ("realistic biological algorithm", IU "Vitamin D Bank", a DANGER state at 400 percent of baseline) and its PRO tier is described at "$1.99" ([listing](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/f8ece1ea-dc33-4b01-8ca0-1d938262d891)). One discrepancy to resolve by eye: the market notes read the listing as a widget, while the store API in the naming notes lists it as a watch app. The report uses the API reading only for counts, not for type. The window wording is wider than SunIQ: "Sun Tracker: UV Vitamin D and Burn Risk" already says "find the next good window to get outside", "SunAlert: UV, Burn & Vitamin D" (4.9 stars from 22 reviews) logs a vitamin D estimate and carries a medical disclaimer, and "Vitamin D Estimate" is a data field ([SunAlert](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/33a60aac-8f33-4ca5-9e94-7fd0a61cac0d?countryCode=US), [Sun Tracker](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/abb277e7-cef1-421e-8682-407fed5f7974?countryCode=US)). Free UV-number widgets are saturated, with the largest at 50K+ downloads ([UV-Index widget](https://apps.garmin.com/en-US/apps/b0776624-e65c-4be1-8854-2cd5c00030ce)).

Demand for vitamin D specifically is anecdotal: the one Connect IQ app-idea thread has zero replies and about 1,000 views ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/app-ideas/164465/vitamin-d-tracker-app-idea)); sunburn avoidance drives most Garmin-side mentions, and SunIQ's own reviewers ask for sunscreen reminders and MIP legibility, not more vitamin D precision. Garmin's native weather glance already shows a UV figure, and no native vitamin D feature exists ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/outdoor-recreation/outdoor-recreation/f/instinct-2-series/299942/new-feature-wish-sun-burn-prevention-alarm/1451387)). The gap that remains is differentiation by restraint: a binary read in under a second, no IU or minutes, no phone proxy (the old UV widget "frequently times out" waiting for one), and a nudge that fires at most once. Whether users prefer binary over numeric is untested; no user research was found. The paid price anchors on Garmin are SunIQ's $1.99 and free competitors, well below phone apps at $10 to $60 per year, so the studio's $2.50 tier is plausible but unproven.

## Keep "vitamin D" out of the title and the watch UI

Garmin's only health-specific rule, 1c, requires regulatory clearance for apps intended to diagnose, treat or prevent "disease or other conditions", otherwise the app "must be... intended for informational purposes only"; 1b also bars apps that "create a false sense of security" ([App Review Guidelines](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/articles/app-review-guidelines/Overview.html)). Four live apps carry "Vitamin D" in the title and were approved, which proves approval is possible, not that it is safe: Garmin may remove any app without citing a rule, and the notes found no case either way. A title is the strongest statement of intended use, and the studio's own rule is to describe what is shown, never what it means for health ([watch-pm rules via kit](file:///Users/mbp/dev/watch-design-kit/skills/watch-pm/SKILL.md)). Wording therefore follows six rules: the sun or sky is the grammatical subject, never the person; no threshold is called good or bad; data is attributed to the watch's own weather; no label or colour is keyed to a reading (so OPEN and CLOSED differ by words and shape, not green and red); one disclaimer sentence ("For information only; not medical advice or a sun-safety tool"); and the same words in title, description, screenshots, watch strings, translations and the site pages. Avoid "optimal", "safe", "good", "enough", "deficiency", "goal", "streak", "burn", "SPF" and any minute count.

The naming shortlist, ranked, from a live store search of the top 120 hits per term (no trademark clearance was run; no domain was registered or checked):

| Rank | Name | Store collision | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sun Window | None; nearest is a 10-download face "Window" | Says what it is; Pro would be "Sun Window Pro"; slug `sun-window` is free in `site/src/apps/`; sunwindow.app and .co have DNS records |
| 2 | Sun Gate | None | Suggests open/closed; less self-explanatory |
| 3 | High Sun | None | Plain solar height; could read as a noon face |
| 4 | Sunny Spell | None | Warm, but sounds like weather |
| 5 | Good Sun | None | Implies a verdict; usable only if no state is ever labelled "good" |
| 6, 7 | Sun Hours, Sun Spell | None | Low distinctiveness; Sun Spell near-duplicates Sunny Spell |

Drop "D Window" and "Sun Window D" (a visible vitamin D reference) and avoid "Sun Time(s)" (four exist), "Sun Slot" (gambling association) and anything with "UV" (implies a safety reading). Because the store has no keyword field and indexes descriptions, keeping the title clean costs discoverability; the owner's two options for the description are A, no "vitamin D" anywhere, or B, one hedged mention in the description body only ("often called the vitamin D window"). I recommend A for the watch UI and screenshots, with B as a deliberate exception for the description only if the owner accepts the guideline 1c judgement call. Garmin's brand terms stay out (no "Body Battery"-style marks).

## Recommended spec: a pure-function glance, and a background nudge in Pro

**Rule.** At any instant, `elevation(now_utc, lat, lon)` is computed offline and compared with one constant. Three states: OPEN (elevation at or above 45 degrees now and the sky filter passes), CLOSED (today's maximum reaches 45 degrees but now is before opening, after closing, or the sky filter fails), and NONE TODAY (today's maximum is below 45 degrees). The window is symmetric about solar noon in solar time, so the computation uses the UTC moment, longitude and the equation of time, never a fixed 10:00 to 15:00 clock window. Displaying window start and end clock times is a design choice that I read as outside the owner's "no minutes" rule, but the owner should confirm that. Solar maths should be the full NOAA formulas in `Double` from days since 2000, geometric elevation with no refraction (refraction is 0.016 degrees at 45), because a Julian date near 2.46 million cannot be held in `Float32` and ignoring the equation of time costs up to 4 degrees ([fixtures](../research_notes/Vitamin%20D%20window/solar_elevation_fixtures.md)). The glance and app then need no background process at all: they compute live from stored location, the clock and the cached weather, which also removes the stale-after-midnight failure HeroSet found.

**Data sources by device tier.**

| Tier | Devices | Weather input | Nudge route | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 66 watch products at API 5.1+ | Hourly `uvIndex` and `cloudCover`, plus condition and precipitation chance | `Notifications.showNotification` from a watch-app | Recommended v1 target |
| A-lean | Three Instinct ids (Instinct 3 Solar 45/50 mm, Instinct E 40/45 mm; 4 SKUs) | Same, in 32 KB glance and background pools, 128 KB app | Same, if it fits | Needs lean glance or no glance; fit unproven |
| B | API 3.2 to 5.0 (Venu 2, FR945/745/245, fēnix 6) | Condition and precipitation only | `requestApplicationWake` yes/no dialog (API 2.3.0), behaviour varies by watch | Defer; SunIQ also excludes fēnix 6 for its window screen |
| C | No weather | None; elevation rule only, no "worse later" | None | Not worth a separate build |

The notification module's SDK page lists 57 device entries and omits the FR70, FR170 and fēnix 9 families that the device files put at API 5.1+; the studio's precedent is to trust device data over prose, and the upload form's Compatible Devices list is the final check.

**Manifest.** Type `watch-app` with `getGlanceView`; `minApiLevel` 5.1 for any build that uses notifications or UV; permissions `Positioning` (for the one-time location), plus `Background` and `Notifications` in the Pro build only; no `Communications`, no `SensorHistory`. `UserProfile` is added only if quiet hours read sleep and wake times. The studio's own privacy line applies: while `Positioning` is declared, no "no location" copy.

**Location.** Call `Position.getInfo()` in the foreground, round to 0.1 degree (about 11 km, negligible for elevation), store it with a timestamp, and let glance and background read Storage. Do not depend on `Activity.currentLocation` or the Weather observation location. If the app was never opened, the glance shows a plain "open the app once" state.

**Nudge logic (Pro).** Register one temporal event, starting around 15 minutes (the floor is 5), and run a cheap gate first: if today has no window or it is closed, exit. If it is open, snapshot `(now, steps)` into Storage. "Inactive too long" is a step delta below a threshold over roughly 60 to 90 minutes of daylight (use `pushes` when wheelchair mode zeroes `steps`); `moveBarLevel >= 1` can corroborate but its meaning (about one hour of stillness) is forum-grade ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/5379/how-to-display-movementbar-in-watchface)). "Forecast worse later" compares the current hour with the remaining hourly entries inside today's window, using `uvIndex`, `cloudCover`, precipitation chance and the rain-family conditions, and is limited to about 12 hours and the same day. Fire at most once per window and once per day, never during an activity (a forum report says accepting a wake prompt mid-activity can end the activity ([thread](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/discussion/239715/background-notifications-stop-ongoing-activity))), keep an in-app off switch that survives updates, and store all nudge policy in Storage because a declined wake dialog never reaches the app. Background code cannot write `Properties`, only `Storage`. Copy describes the sky, not the person: "Sun window is open. Cloud from 14:00." Do not promise a buzz; the notification's vibration is the watch's decision. All numeric thresholds above (polling interval, step threshold, cloud band) are starting values to tune on a wrist, not researched figures.

**Free versus Pro (proposal).** Because Free and Pro are separate app ids built from one codebase, the cleanest split also separates the permission surface.

| Capability | Free "Sun Window" | Pro "Sun Window Pro" |
|---|---|---|
| Glance and app: OPEN / CLOSED / NONE TODAY, window start and end times | Yes | Yes |
| Accent colour (list only, append-only ids) | Yes (studio rule) | Yes |
| Weather demotion (UV below 3 or heavy cloud demotes OPEN) | Yes, where `uvIndex`/`cloudCover` exist | Yes |
| Hourly "worse later" line in the app | No | Yes |
| Background nudge, `Background` and `Notifications` permissions | No | Yes |
| "Sun height" list (45 / 40 / 35 degrees) | No | Optional; below about 35 degrees no source supports a synthesis claim |
| Price | Free | The $2.50 tier, set in the upload form; no price number in listing text |

The advantage of keeping the nudge in Pro is that Free asks only for location, has no battery or review risk from background work, and still beats a bare UV number. The disadvantage is that the signature feature is paywalled, which only the owner can weigh. A later Pro indicator inside TwoSuns is possible, reusing its location and weather code, but faces cannot host the wake prompt, so it would be a glanceable indicator, not a nudge.

## Test fixtures for the solar maths

The full reference tables live in [`solar_elevation_fixtures.md`](../research_notes/Vitamin%20D%20window/solar_elevation_fixtures.md) and its generator [`solar_elevation_fixtures.py`](../research_notes/Vitamin%20D%20window/solar_elevation_fixtures.py), all 2026. The NOAA algorithm agrees with JPL Horizons to within 0.0061 degrees over 51,876 samples and with MET Norway's noon elevation to within 0.008 degrees ([NOAA technical details](https://gml.noaa.gov/grad/solcalc/calcdetails.html), [JPL Horizons](https://ssd.jpl.nasa.gov/api/horizons.api)). Values worth asserting, with latitude and longitude from the file (Vilnius 54.687, 25.280; London 51.507, -0.128; Phoenix 33.448, -112.074; Reykjavik 64.147, -21.940; Singapore 1.352, 103.820; Sydney -33.868, 151.209):

| Case | Expected | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Vilnius 2026-06-21 12:00 local (+3), 09:00 UTC | 55.33 degrees; 45 degree window 10:26 to 16:16; max 58.751 at 13:20:41 | Open day, DST offset |
| Vilnius 2026-03-20 | max 35.244, no 45 degree window (30 degree window 10:28 to 14:26) | NONE TODAY case |
| Vilnius 2026-12-21 12:00 local (+2) | 11.791 | Deep winter |
| London 2026-03-20 12:00 UTC | 38.422 | Near-equinox, NONE TODAY |
| Phoenix 2026-12-21 12:00 local (-7), 19:00 UTC | 32.765 | Low sun, warm place |
| Reykjavik 2026-07-15 12:00 UTC | 44.565 (max 47.315; 45 degree window 12:08 to 14:59) | Just under threshold at noon clock, still a window day |
| Singapore 2026-03-20 13:00 local (+8) | 86.588 (max 88.493) | `acos` near 1 numerical edge; not tested in `Float32` |
| Sydney 2026-09-23 10:00 local (+10) | 47.752 | Southern hemisphere, AEST |

Date-range assertions need a tolerance: a 0.5 degree error moves a range edge 1 to 3 days (Reykjavik 2 to 3), 1.0 degree moves it 3 to 5. Use dates well inside or outside (Vilnius 2026-05-15 inside; 2026-03-20 and 2026-10-15 outside), and never assert exact edge dates: Sydney 2026-04-19 reads 44.9995 degrees in the reference and 45.0000 in Horizons, so in-or-out is undecidable. Tolerances for a `Double` port of the full NOAA formulas are 0.02 degrees and 1 minute on window edges. A Spencer-series shortcut has a measured maximum error of 0.457 degrees (12 yes/no verdict differences over 2,190 place-days) and needs 0.6 degrees and 10 minutes; the cruder Cooper variants fail a 45 degree rule. These are 2026-only and `Float32` was not measured, so a Monkey C test on the simulator is still needed, and a watch run is the only device proof.

## What could go wrong

The largest risk is the unproven background chain: if temporal events do not fire for a watch-app, or the Weather cache returns null inside a background process, the Pro nudge fails silently, and a one-day spike on the FR965 would find that before any design investment. The second is claim risk: a visible OPEN state is read by some users as a recommendation, and by others as a sun-safety signal, which is guideline 1b's "false sense of security"; the mitigation is describe-never-claim wording, a disclaimer, and no colour or label keyed to a reading. Third, retention and relevance: in northern Europe NONE TODAY dominates for 7.5 months, and the 100-download SunIQ and 1,000-download Vitamin D Estimate suggest the paid pool is a few hundred people. Fourth, nudge annoyance: Garmin's move alert is the best-documented failure (it clears while sitting, cannot be turned off, keeps turning on ([forum](https://forums.garmin.com/sports-fitness/sports-fitness/f/forerunner-935/135896/can-t-turn-off-move-alert))), and the notes found no Garmin rate limit, so the once-a-day cap is policy, not platform. Fifth, permissions: adding `Positioning` or `Notifications` costs trust, how they appear at install is unverified, and whether an update re-prompts existing users is an open question in the studio's notes. Sixth, battery: no Garmin figure exists and review guideline 2a ("do not hurt battery") is the only constraint, so a wear-day comparison is required before any battery claim. Last, the Instinct 32 KB pools and 1-bit display may force a lean glance or exclude those watches.

## Conclusion

The unexpected result is that the platform is not the obstacle and the science is not the obstacle either; the obstacle is that a sun window is a convention with a clean, testable core and an unusually thin market. Because elevation can be computed offline to hundredths of a degree and a glance can be a pure function of clock, location and cached weather, the Free build is cheap, low-permission and mostly independent of the unproven background chain, which is exactly why it is the right first slice. The Pro nudge is where all the uncertainty sits, so it should earn its place through a one-day spike rather than a spec.

The strategic implication is that the studio's constraints are also its differentiator and its discoverability cost: no numbers, no vitamin D claim and no colour keyed to a reading make the product calmer and safer than SunIQ, and also harder to find by the one search term the audience uses. If the owner accepts that trade, builds the Free glance first and lets the spike decide the nudge, the downside is a small build; if the aim is revenue, the evidence says the paid sun niche on Garmin is a few hundred people, and this app should be sized accordingly.
