# Repair listings, prove accuracy, then build

**As of 2026-09-25, the studio's next moves are cheap, unglamorous and sequential: repair both store listings, prove HeroSet counts better than Garmin's free native counter, and answer four Garmin/dashboard questions before writing any new feature.** HeroSet 1.1.1 and HeroFace 1.0.1 are both live with **1 and 0 downloads and 0 reviews** ([HeroSet](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/54bbf625-82af-4715-8af0-f2f16a5d1377?countryCode=US), [HeroFace](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps/ad04d1e1-8e30-45cb-bbd6-82374f77b116?countryCode=US)), yet almost none of the free listing levers the research found are applied: HeroSet still sits in category 219 instead of Strength Training, and HeroFace's live description is **166 characters**. The single largest strategic risk is that HeroSet's differentiator, counting accuracy, is unproven: the launch gate for it was waived (ADR-042) and only one 35-rep set exists. The corrections that change decisions are: break-even is **~59 sales a year, not 48**; paid watch apps are **54 of 120, not 103**; paid faces are **34 of 120, not 51**; Ready cannot exist as a watch app; anything below CIQ 3.4 cannot be sold; and the "80 watches" site claim (117 for HeroFace) now exceeds what the store lists (66 and 69). The code review is essentially closed in code; what remains is proof on hardware (nothing below has device evidence beyond the FR965) and two zero-code checks, the original-Venu burn-in rule and the site-versus-store device counts. Nothing in the notes supports building a new product yet: no demand signal exists for either live app, and a new listing restarts a 6-24 month traction clock.

## What is verified, what is only simulated, and what nobody knows

The verified core is Garmin's own rules and the store's shape. Garmin keeps **15% of the tax-exclusive price**, charges a **$100 non-refundable annual fee**, pays out monthly at a **$10 minimum** after the 48-hour return window, and limits merchants by country ([App Sales](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/articles/monetization/App_Sales.html), [Merchant Onboarding](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/articles/monetization/Merchant_Onboarding.html)) [verified 2026-09-25]. Web purchasing ended on **2025-11-20**; the Connect IQ mobile app is the sole route to buy or install ([Garmin announcement](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/b/news-announcements/posts/changes-to-the-connect-iq-store)) [verified]. Trials exist for apps but only through a developer-run HTTPS unlock server, and "not for watch faces" ([Trial Apps](https://developer.garmin.com/connect-iq/articles/core-topics/Trial_Apps.html)) [verified]. Rank lists cap at 120 rows, and rank is not lifetime installs: #1 Goals sits in the 100k bucket, #2 Face It in 5M ([store pull](https://apps.garmin.com/api/appsLibraryExternalServices/api/asw/apps?startPageIndex=0&pageSize=30&sortType=mostPopular&countryCode=US&appType=WATCHFACE)) [verified]. Of the top 120 faces **34 are paid, 99 are over a year old, 3 are under 90 days, none has trial mode**, and only 2 of the 34 paid faces reach the 100k bucket while 26 sit at 10k [verified]. New apps at zero downloads on day three are normal [verified locally, feed skews to last week]. No indie has published Connect IQ revenue [verified negative finding].

The best-evidenced market gap is execution, not category. Across 2,544 one- and two-star reviews of the top 30 faces, device fit (14.8%), paywall or key friction (14.4%), and settings that do not save (8.4%) dominate; accessibility, "calm minimal", HRV and Training Readiness do not [verified 2026-09-22 from the review corpus; 94% of reviews are for superseded versions, so treat shares as floors]. HeroFace already sits on that gap with one honest $2.00 price and a 10-size fit suite. What no note can supply is demand: there is no conversion, revenue or ARPU figure anywhere, and no evidence that a goal-bar-plus-streak face converts. In the fitness niche the incumbent Push-Up Hero has **4.3 stars over 905 reviews**, with 63 one-star texts almost all about counting accuracy; nobody sells one multi-exercise app that counts well [verified locally]. Meanwhile the Strength Training shelf is 101 apps with **13 of the top 20 free**, already past the research's own 40% "real pressure" threshold [verified 2026-09-25].

Everything engineering-side marked fixed is fixed in code and simulator only. The code review's fix list is essentially complete: all High and Medium items are fixed in the repo at HEAD `4791782`, the staged-site hazard (W1) was avoided because the "80 watches" copy reached `main` after 1.1.1 went live, and only the original-Venu burn-in item (F1/F6), part of the CSP (W12), the HeroFace screenshots (W14) and low-value hygiene remain. But HeroSet 1.1.1 shipped with device checks deferred by owner decision (ADR-050), so the dashboard START fix on `d2airx10`, the `onKey` commit path (which has no automated test because `KeyEvent` cannot be constructed), and touch behaviour on 13 products are **live but unverified on hardware**. Only the FR965 has run either app. Simulator passing is not device proof, and every "fixed" in this report inherits that limit.

Five things stay unknown and are carried forward as gaps, not upgraded: whether listing text still drives discovery inside the **mobile** app (the web endpoint is verified; the mobile app's use of it is not); whether paid listings are hidden from search on fēnix 9 and FR170 (a single unanswered forum report, [thread 444114](https://forums.garmin.com/developer/connect-iq/f/connect-iq-web-store/444114/paid-listings-filtered-out-of-store-search-on-58-of-115-devices-including-forerunner-170-and-fenix-9-is-there-a-published-monetization-device-list)); what Garmin does to reviews and downloads on a paid-to-free switch (its docs are silent); whether a text-only listing edit needs re-review or resets the release date; and whether adding a permission re-prompts existing users. Native rep-count accuracy on current hardware is also unmeasured; the 3.0-67.5% error figure comes from four older watches with an unconfirmed study year [plausible at best].

## Where the notes disagree, and which one wins

**Break-even, 48 versus ~59 versus ~60.** The 48 came from multiplying a tax-inclusive 2.49 EUR by 0.85; Garmin splits the tax-exclusive price point, so $2.00 x 0.85 = $1.70 and the fee needs 58.8 sales. The market note's "about 60" is the same number rounded. I trust **59**, and it is per merchant year because the fee is annual. It is still optimistic: FX and digital service taxes are withheld, and payouts arrive separately from Garmin International and Garmin Europe, so the $10 minimum may bite per entity for a mostly European buyer base [plausible, unverified].

**HeroFace 1.0.1 live status.** Three notes read the store API today and see 1.0.1 (changed around 2026-09-24 15:41 UTC); the code-review note repeats the local doc's "still 1.0.0". I trust the API, and the doc is the stale party. What is still not done is the description: the live text remains the 166-character version. So the fixes F2, F3, F4 and F10 are live but simulator-verified only.

**Why devices are missing from the store.** The go-to-market note says Garmin's paid-app allow-list largely explains it; the opportunities note agrees only partly. Both are right. The allow-list (API 6.0, 5.2, 5.1, 5.0 and one 3.4 set, "subject to change") accounts for HeroFace's CIQ 3.x gaps and HeroSet's fēnix 6S, Enduro 1 and FR945 LTE. It does **not** account for Descent MK2/MK2S, MARQ Gen 1 and D2 Air X10, which are on the list yet absent, while FR70 and FR170 are shown though unnamed in the SDK tiers. The raw API counts (93 and 96 device-type IDs) are a different unit from 66 and 69 products, so they settle nothing. Unresolved, and only Garmin can close it; ask about those three families specifically.

**Paid-share numbers.** These are not in conflict; they measure different populations. Faces: 34 of 120 paid, so 86 free, and the free ones hold the 500k-5M buckets. Watch apps: 54 of 120 paid. Strength Training shelf: 27 paid of 101. Use the population that matches the decision: for HeroFace price, the face census; for HeroSet, the shelf (73% free).

**Is search still a lever?** The market note trusts the live `/apps/keywords` endpoint; the go-to-market note flags that one forum poster says Garmin removed app search after the web store closed. Endpoint verified, mobile application unverified. HeroSet ranks **#3 of 982 for "rep counter" at zero to one installs**, so listing text plainly works on the web index. Treat "listing repair moves rank" as the best available hypothesis and test it on a phone before investing beyond the free edits.

**FR965 API level.** The compatible-devices page and SDK profile say 5.2.0; HeroSet's own log records a crash on "CIQ 6.0.2". Runtime firmware may report newer than the compile baseline. Plan on 5.2: anything needing 6.0 (Sleep Score) cannot be dogfooded on the wrist. Unresolved.

**Sequencing between notes.** The go-to-market note wants listing repair with a real version bump (HeroSet 1.1.2, or fold into 1.2.0 only if it ships within two weeks); the opportunities note ranks Connect sync 1.2.0 first and Instinct AMOLED as a bundle for the next upload. I merge them: first find out whether the dashboard edits category and description in place, since that may need no binary at all; do not delay the listing waiting for 1.2.0; add the three Instinct AMOLED products only after Garmin answers the device-list question, because a manifest addition the store then filters buys zero reach.

## Open owner decisions

**Accuracy claims versus the release contract.** `release-contract.md` forbids promising measured counting accuracy and gate 2 was waived. The go-to-market note's stop-rule (if HeroSet does not beat native, stop all marketing and fix the detector) is currently unevaluated. My recommendation: run the test before amending anything; publish method only, without numbers, unless HeroSet wins, and only then amend the contract.

**Measuring outcomes versus "no analytics".** The site promises zero client JS and no analytics. Recommended: keep the promise this month, attribute by store-dashboard install timing and per-channel redirect counts, and postpone the landing-to-store handoff metric; at these volumes nothing else is readable anyway.

**Category: fix in place or in the next upload.** The README and the live listing disagree; the dashboard decides.

**What the site's device count means.** Options are "manifest count", or "N in the store today, see Compatible Devices". The list is "subject to change", so prefer a link over a number. This is not the W1 timing problem; it is the 80-versus-66 and 117-versus-69 mismatch.

**Instinct AMOLED for HeroSet.** Confirm the exclusion was only about the semi-octagon cut-out. If so, three products are a half-day change.

**Policy on shipping with owed device checks.** ADR-050 made it an owner call; a durable rule (which checks block a release) would stop this being re-argued each time.

**Deferred with triggers, no action now:** a free HeroFace twin (reach argument: a free listing is not confined to the paid allow-list, though it restarts reviews; wait for Garmin's paid-to-free answer and the 30-day ratio); Cyrillic fallback (document as a deliberate `system-ui` fallback, five minutes); an on-watch settings path for HeroFace (editor exposure to third-party faces unverified).

## Never build, or cut

**Never:** Pace Face (most crowded category, wrong API names); Ready as a watch app (platform-blocked, medical-claim clause, and the release contract forbids medical claims); any paid product aimed below CIQ 3.4; a fourth HeroSet exercise; day history and best-set-today unless the owner overrides with new reasoning; a second HeroSet complication; an in-app trial (ADR-039, needs a payment-style backend); clones of Garmin stock faces; accessibility, calm-minimal, Training Readiness/HRV, women's health, CGM, golf and swim positioning; full-brightness always-on, live compass and weather radar.

**Cut or merge:** Rank Face (duplicates HeroFace's HeroSet mode, and HeroSet has about one download); Verden Habits and Field Face (no-go until the funnel ratio proves a second product carries; if a second face is ever built, make it a free HeroFace); phone-disconnected, DND and moon icons; the approaching-goal haptic; barrel kits until a second app exists; a family accent ADR and studio page (the real fix is a link in HeroFace's description); the seconds half of the draw-bypass fix and the divide guard (behaviour-neutral); flipping `noUncheckedIndexedAccess`; chasing `-l 3` to zero.

**Do not this month:** flip either listing to free, rename HeroSet, cancel the merchant account (it demonetizes both apps and requires repaying the fee), or spend on ads. A $75 test buys roughly 2-9 installs and cannot show the product sells. Do not quote 103/120, 51/120 or 48 sales in any doc.

## Conclusion

The research's largest correction is one of sequence, not fact: the studio has been thinking in terms of what to build next, while the evidence says the next unit of value is inside two existing listings and one measurement. Search is the only surface a zero-install newcomer controls, and it is free; the accuracy result is the only thing that decides whether HeroSet deserves marketing at all; the rest of the local backlog is either done, blocked by the platform, or below the noise floor.

The realistic year-one result is a small loss against the $100 fee, and the notes give no route to the 100k tier that only two paid faces reach. That makes month 12 a deliberate renewal decision rather than a default, and it makes the Garmin answers (paid to free, device list) the highest-leverage emails the studio can send. The two open risks that could still surprise a live customer are both cheap to close this week: the original-Venu burn-in rule in the simulator, and the device counts the site claims but the store does not list.
